AI search in 2026: a dated timeline and the statistics that hold up

A dated, sourced reference for AI search in 2026: 28 statistics on usage, B2B buyers, clicks, accuracy, and variance; 24 events from September 2025 to September 2026; and 18 widely repeated numbers to stop quoting, with what to cite instead. Every figure carries its number, date, source, and sample, and each was checked against its source on Sept. 26, 2026. The pattern: use of AI answers kept growing, clicks to websites kept falling, and the numbers that travel furthest are often the least scoped.

Direct answer

As of Sept. 26, 2026, the AI search numbers that hold up are dated and scoped: ChatGPT has more than 1 billion weekly active users (OpenAI, Aug. 31, 2026), Google reports more than 2.5 billion monthly users for AI Overviews and more than 1 billion monthly active users for AI Mode (Google, May 19, 2026), and 49% of U.S. adults use AI chatbots (Pew, June 17, 2026).

What fell was the click, not the question: Alphabet said in April that Search queries were at an all-time high, while Chartbeat data show Google Search referrals to publishers down 40.2% in the year to July 2026. B2B buyers moved too, but less completely than the headlines suggest: 94% use AI somewhere in buying (Forrester), 45% used generative AI mainly to research vendors (Gartner), and review sites still narrowly beat chatbots as the top shortlist influence (G2).

Below are 28 statistics, each with its number, date, source, and sample; 24 dated events from September 2025 to September 2026; and 18 widely repeated numbers to stop quoting, with what to cite instead.

How this page chooses its numbers

Every figure here passed four tests. It comes from the organization that produced it, or, where the original is gated, from a named outlet that reported it (marked "via"). It carries the date of the data or the statement. Its sample and scope are stated. And we checked it against its source on Sept. 26, 2026. Usage counts from OpenAI and Google are labeled company-reported, survey results are what respondents said, and arXiv papers not yet peer reviewed are marked as preprints.

We left out statistics from AEO and SEO tool vendors' own studies, including SolCrys's; our own citation study appears only as a qualitative, engine-by-engine cross-check. Vendors publish useful research, but they sell the thing their research measures, and several of the most repeated vendor figures don't survive a look at the method (see the stop-quoting table). Three figures below touch a vendor's data, and each is labeled: Adobe's retail figures come from Adobe Analytics, and Adobe also sells AEO products; SparkToro's zero-click analysis uses Similarweb's panel; and the Reddit citation figures Search Engine Journal reported came from a GEO tracking tool. We keep them because each rests on a large measured sample or was published and scoped by a neutral party.

Reading the usage numbers

Units decide the story. ChatGPT reports weekly users, Google reports monthly users, and AI Mode's October 2025 figure was daily. A monthly count is always at least as large as a weekly or daily count of the same audience, so the rise from 75 million to 1 billion for AI Mode is partly a change of unit.

AI Overviews appear inside ordinary Google results, so their user count is best read as exposure to AI answers, not a choice to use an AI tool. The independent surveys put the consumer figures in proportion: about half of U.S. adults use chatbots (Pew), and 10% of people across 48 markets use them for news each week (Reuters Institute).

Reading the buyer numbers

Forrester's 94% counts any AI use during buying. Gartner's 45% asks whether buyers used generative AI mainly to gather vendor and product information, in a smaller sample fielded in August and September 2025. When a claim is about vendor research specifically, the narrower figure is the one that fits.

G2 surveys software buyers and runs a software review marketplace. Its April and July reports point in different directions on the same question, which is a reason to cite them together, not to drop either. The verification step hasn't gone away: 69% of buyers prefer to check AI output with a sales rep, and only 9% would let an agent complete a purchase. Reading the traffic numbers

Clicks fell, not queries. Alphabet reported Search queries "at an all-time high" on April 29, 2026, and the decline shows up in referrals instead: Pew's 2025 baseline found a result was clicked on 8% of visits with an AI summary against 15% without one, and publisher data for 2025 and 2026 show double-digit drops.

Publisher data is not your data. The Chartbeat and Clarity samples are news and publisher sites. A B2B software site with branded demand can follow a different curve, so check your own Search Console before borrowing a publisher number; this diagnostic shows how.

Conversion claims flip. In Adobe's retail data, AI-referred shoppers converted 38% worse than other traffic in March 2025 and 42% better in March 2026. A conversion multiplier for AI traffic without a vertical and a date is not a finding; the dependable number is your own, and closed-won interviews catch the influence analytics can't see.

Reading the accuracy numbers

Every accuracy figure is engine- and topic-specific. The 11.0% describes Google AI Overviews on trending queries, not ChatGPT or Perplexity, and not questions about one brand. The 64% is buyers' perception. Neither is an error rate for your company; that takes your own prompts, run repeatedly and graded against facts you can document (see whether AI is telling the truth about your brand).

Variance is a design constraint, not a reason to give up. Identical lists are rare, but rates across many runs are stable enough to track, and how many runs a trustworthy number takes depends on the size of the difference you need to detect.

Sources move for reasons outside your content. ChatGPT's Reddit citations fell sharply in September 2025 and again in August 2026, on that one engine, and OpenAI did not comment on the August drop. A source's AI visibility can change with retrieval or licensing decisions you never see. And no technique has yet shown a stable causal effect across platforms, so treat any "X raises AI visibility by N%" claim as a hypothesis to test on your own prompts.

What the timeline shows

Three patterns run through the year. Answers started carrying ads: ChatGPT's ads went from a U.S. test in February to a reported $1 billion run rate by Aug. 31, while Anthropic said Claude will stay ad-free. Buying inside the chat retreated while discovery grew: Instant Checkout was phased out and Atlas shut down, while OpenAI refocused ChatGPT shopping on product discovery. And free platform reports arrived that measure presence, not outcomes: Bing reports citations without clicks, GA4's AI channel leaves out Google's own AI surfaces, and Search Console reports AI impressions only.

How to cite an AI search statistic responsibly

Four fields make a statistic citable: source, date, sample, and scope. Three more keep it honest: unit, interest, and kind. These are the same rules in our published editorial standards.

  • Source: the originator, not the aggregator. Link the study or statement that produced the number. Statistics roundups drop the scope, which is how a news-only zero-click figure becomes a claim about all searches.
  • Date: when the data was collected, as well as when it was published. Pew's click study was fielded in March 2025 and published in July 2025. A 2024 prediction is not a 2026 fact.
  • Sample: who, and how many. A survey of 645 buyers and one of 18,000 can both be right about different questions. One company's website is not a sample.
  • Scope: engine, surface, geography, device, and vertical. An AI Overviews finding is not a ChatGPT finding, a U.S. retail conversion rate is not a B2B software rate, and a web panel that excludes the Google app misses much of mobile search.
  • Unit: daily, weekly, or monthly; share of answers or share of citations; relative or absolute change. "Down 86%" and "down 3.3 points" can describe the same drop.
  • Interest: who benefits. Platforms report their own usage, review sites survey buyers about review sites, and tool vendors publish studies that sell tools. Disclose it; it doesn't make a number wrong.
  • Kind: measurement, self-report, or prediction. Say which. A survey answer describes what people say they do; a panel describes what a sample did; a forecast describes neither.

The same fact, cited two ways

Weak: "AI Overviews have 2.5 billion users." Citable: "Google said on May 19, 2026, that AI Overviews had more than 2.5 billion monthly users (company-reported)."

Weak: "AI traffic converts 4x better." Citable: "In Adobe Analytics data on U.S. retail sites, AI-referred traffic converted 42% better than other traffic in March 2026, after converting 38% worse a year earlier (TechCrunch, April 16, 2026)."

How SolCrys applies these rules

SolCrys is an AEO platform built around Measure → Diagnose → Execute → Verify, and the discipline on this page shapes both what it measures and what it declines to claim.

  • Engine by engine. SolCrys runs a fixed set of buyer prompts on ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Gemini, Perplexity, and Claude, and keeps each engine's results separate. That is how the August 2026 Reddit break showed up as one engine's change in our 36,268-citation study of the AEO category, which states its window, prompt count, engines, and method.
  • Rates, not ranks. In line with the variance research above, SolCrys's core metrics are rates across repeated runs, not a position in a single answer.
  • Observations, not proof. After a team ships a change, Verify re-runs the same frozen prompt set on the same engines and, for a page fixed from a Content Audit, re-audits the page. A before-and-after number is reported as an observation, not as proof of cause, which is the bar the 45-study survey sets.
  • Accuracy with evidence. Answer Accuracy grades AI answers against your organization's Corporate Context and cannot mark a claim wrong without pointing to the evidence.

What SolCrys doesn't measure

SolCrys measures answers, not visits. FAQ

How many people use ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, and AI Overviews in 2026?

ChatGPT has more than 1 billion weekly active users (OpenAI, Aug. 31, 2026). Google reports more than 2.5 billion monthly users for AI Overviews and more than 1 billion monthly active users for AI Mode (May 19, 2026, repeated for AI Mode on July 22), and 950 million monthly users for the Gemini app (July 22, 2026). All are company-reported, and the units differ: weekly for ChatGPT, monthly for Google.

How much do Google AI Overviews reduce clicks?

The clearest neutral measurement is Pew's: in March 2025, people clicked a traditional result on 8% of visits when an AI summary appeared and 15% when none did, and clicked a link inside the summary on 1%. Publisher data since then show larger falls in Google referrals overall, including a 40.2% year-over-year decline to July 2026 in Chartbeat's network, but those figures cover more than AI Overviews and come from news sites.

What share of B2B buyers use AI to research vendors?

It depends on the question asked. Forrester found 94% of business buyers use AI somewhere in the buying process. Gartner found 45% used generative AI primarily to gather vendor and product information, and 69% prefer to validate AI output with sales reps. G2 found 51% of software buyers start research in a chatbot more often than in Google (April 2026), and that review sites (38%) narrowly edged chatbots (37%) as the top shortlist influence (July 2026).

Does AI search traffic convert better than organic search?

Sometimes, and not consistently. In Adobe's U.S. retail data, AI-referred traffic converted 42% better than other traffic in March 2026 but 38% worse in March 2025. Microsoft Clarity found AI referrals converted at up to 3 times the rate of search and social on more than 1,200 publisher and news sites, in 1 month of data, while staying under 1% of visits. The widely shared 4.4x and 23x multipliers come from a vendor study that doesn't disclose its data and from one company's own website. Measure your own with GA4's AI Assistant channel, which excludes Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode.

Which AI search statistics are wrong or outdated?

The most common: Gartner's 2024 prediction of a 25% drop in search volume by 2026 (queries grew; clicks fell), "AI Mode grew from 75 million to 1 billion users" (daily vs. monthly), "ChatGPT hit 1 billion weekly users on July 31" (that was a cross-product count; the weekly figure dates from Aug. 31), "AI is wrong 45% of the time" (news questions in mid-2025), and "Search Console shows AI clicks" (impressions only). The full table, with what to cite instead, is on this page.

How accurate are AI search answers?

On Google AI Overviews, a 2026 study found that 11.0% of 98,020 claims were not supported by the pages cited for them, with omission the most common failure (preprint). Separately, 64% of B2B software buyers told G2 they often or very often get inaccurate AI chatbot recommendations. Neither is an error rate for a specific brand; that takes repeated runs of your own prompts, graded against facts you can document.

Can AI visibility be measured if answers change every time?

Yes, as a rate. SparkToro found less than a 1 in 100 chance that 2 runs return the same brand list, but concluded that visibility percentage across many prompts run multiple times is a reasonable metric. A rank position in a single answer is not.

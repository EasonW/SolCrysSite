What a ChatGPT visibility tracker measures

Three signals matter for AI search the way blue-link rank and click-through rate matter for traditional SEO. A useful tracker measures all three on the same set of buyer-intent prompts, so the metrics are comparable over time and across engines.

  • Mention rate — across N prompts, how often does ChatGPT name your brand at all? This is the AI-search equivalent of impressions: if ChatGPT never mentions you, no downstream metric matters.
  • Share of voice — of all brand mentions across the same N prompts, what percentage belong to you vs. each competitor? This is the AI-search equivalent of paid-search share of voice — a relative, head-to-head metric that travels across category and time.
  • Citation sources — when ChatGPT names your brand, which URLs is it citing? Owned domains, customer review sites, Reddit threads, news articles, or competitor pages? Citation-source analysis tells you where to invest content effort to actually shift mention rate.

How a ChatGPT visibility tracker differs from SEO tools

Traditional SEO tools (Ahrefs, SEMrush, Moz, SE Ranking) measure URL rank on Google's keyword results page. Their core unit of analysis is *your URL* and its position on a SERP. A ChatGPT visibility tracker has a different unit of analysis: *your brand name as a string token* inside an AI-generated answer. The signals don't substitute for each other:

  • SEO measures URL rank. AI visibility measures whether your brand name appears in an answer paragraph at all. You can rank #1 on Google for a query and never get mentioned by ChatGPT on the same query.
  • SEO is keyword-driven. AI visibility is prompt-driven. A tracker has to run actual prompts through a live model; you can't infer mention rate from a keyword-volume database.
  • SEO clicks are countable. AI mentions are countable but referral-link signals are sparse. The right metric for AI search is mention rate and share of voice, not session counts.
  • SEO tools optimize content for crawlers. AI visibility trackers surface what AI engines *already think* about your brand — including hallucinations and stale claims that need content correction.

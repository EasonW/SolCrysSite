Businesses in ChatGPT: what claiming your business changes, and what it doesn't

OpenAI now lets eligible businesses claim a ChatGPT profile, much as Google does. What claiming changes in ChatGPT's answers, and what the new Insights count, is narrower than the launch suggests.

Updated 2026-11-10

Questions this guide answers

  • What is Businesses in ChatGPT?
  • How do I claim my business in ChatGPT?
  • Does claiming my business in ChatGPT improve visibility?
  • What do ChatGPT Business Hub insights show?

Direct answer

On Nov. 10, 2026, OpenAI opened Businesses in ChatGPT in beta. In Business Hub, which opens from ChatGPT's sidebar, an eligible business can claim its profile, edit its details, connect a product feed, and see Insights. Eligible US ecommerce and online-service businesses can also publish a Business Agent that answers customers' questions in a labeled chat.

Claim yours if you can. It hands ChatGPT your facts directly, and OpenAI lists no fee. Treat it as an input, not a control: OpenAI says claiming "doesn't guarantee increased visibility or give you direct control over ChatGPT's independent recommendations," and its terms let it "supplement, edit, or replace" what you submit using other sources. Your website and the sources that cite you still shape the answers.

Read the Insights by their definitions: they cover ChatGPT only, count an online business's website rather than its name, and show themes, not prompts or competitors.

What launched, and who can use it

Claiming takes two checks: proof that you control the business (by phone, business email, a DNS TXT record, or Shopify), then an identity check through Persona. Only after both can you publish edits, see Insights, or create an agent. OpenAI reviews each edit, and a published profile can't be deleted.

What the Insights count

Insights cover the past 28 days, and most metrics are narrower than their names.

  • Mentions. In OpenAI's definition, for an online business "a mention counts when ChatGPT references the business's website. The brand name alone doesn't count." An answer that recommends you without citing your site isn't counted.
  • Website clicks. An OpenAI spokesperson told Digiday these are "clicks on links from supported ChatGPT surfaces, not completed visits or purchases."
  • Profile views. Digiday reports the dashboard shows these too; OpenAI hasn't published a definition.
  • Discovery topics. "Common themes in ChatGPT answers that mentioned or cited your business," which OpenAI says "don't reveal individual prompts."
  • Blank metrics. Metrics stay blank below a privacy threshold, and a blank "doesn't necessarily mean zero activity."

What the Insights leave out

Businesses don't see chats, even with their own agent, and the spokesperson said "share of voice isn't part of this beta." So the report can't show which brand ChatGPT recommends when it doesn't recommend you, whether what it says about you is accurate, or how other engines answer the same buyer. Like Search Console's AI report, it counts your own links.

Those questions need answer-side measurement: a fixed set of buyer questions, run on each engine before your Business Hub edits publish and rerun after every change. What to do first

Do these in order.

  • 1. Claim it before someone else does. Claimable profiles attract hijackers: Google said it stopped nearly 8 million attempts in 2021 to claim Business Profiles that didn't belong to the claimant. Have the account's long-term owner claim it, then add teammates or an agency rather than sharing a login.
  • 2. Make the profile match your site. Use the description, category, and policies your website states. ChatGPT "may also use other sources," so a conflicting profile gives it two versions to choose from; a website consistency audit finds conflicts first.
  • 3. Read the terms before connecting a feed. Providing a feed obliges you to let OpenAI's crawlers access your sites, and any text-and-data-mining opt-out you hold "will have no effect" against OpenAI. If you block OpenAI's crawlers today, decide with counsel first.
  • 4. Test an agent before publishing it. OpenAI may draft one for you; nothing goes live until you publish. Ask it your customers' common questions, and fix the information or instructions when it's wrong. The terms make you responsible for its answers "whether or not you provided, reviewed, or approved them."

FAQ

Is Businesses in ChatGPT the new Google Business Profile?

Partly. Both are claimable profiles, but a Business Profile is what a buyer reads, and its performance report shows the search terms people used. ChatGPT writes each answer itself.

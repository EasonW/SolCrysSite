Attribution & ROI

Search Console, Bing, and GA4 now report AI search. Here's what each shows, and what none of them do.

Google and Microsoft now report AI search for free. Search Console counts impressions of your links in AI Overviews and AI Mode, Bing Webmaster Tools counts Copilot citations and grounding queries, GA4 groups visits from AI assistants, and Merchant Center shows product share of voice in Google's AI shopping answers. Turn on every one that applies to you, because they are first-party data you can't get anywhere else. But each one counts your own links or visits on its own surfaces, so none shows what an answer said, whether it recommended you, whether it was accurate, or what ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Claude told a buyer who never clicked.

Direct answer

The 4 free reports most teams should turn on are Search Console's generative AI performance report (impressions of your links in Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, no clicks), Bing Webmaster Tools AI Performance (citations of your pages in Microsoft Copilot and Bing's AI summaries, no clicks), GA4's AI Assistant channel (visits that arrive with a recognized AI referrer, excluding AI Overviews and AI Mode), and Merchant Center's AI performance insights (product share of voice in AI Mode and AI Overviews, in 5 countries). Use every one that applies to you: they are the only data that comes from the platforms themselves, and they measure real users.

What none of them does is show the answer: what it said about you, whether it recommended you or a competitor, whether it was accurate, or anything ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Claude said to a buyer who didn't click. That half needs answer-side measurement on a fixed set of buyer prompts.

How to read the table

Every free report counts something the platform can see from its own side: links to your site that it showed, citations of your pages, or sessions that reached your site. That is presence and traffic on the platform's own surfaces. It is useful, it is free, and it is real user data rather than a sample of prompts. It is not the answer.

The pattern matters more than any single row. Because the reports count your URLs and your visits, an answer that names you through a review site is invisible to them, and because each platform reports only on itself, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Claude reach your data only as the occasional referral visit.

Search Console: the generative AI performance report

Google announced the report on June 3, 2026, for a subset of sites, and its help page says it reached all websites worldwide on Aug. 31, 2026. It counts impressions, defined as "how many times links to your site were shown to a user in a generative AI feature on Google Search," across AI Overviews and AI Mode. A separate version covers AI features in Discover.

You can group impressions by page, country, date, and device, and filter by 2 search types, text-based and multimodal. There is no clicks metric, no CTR, and no query dimension, so you can see which pages appeared but not which questions triggered them. Search Labs experiments are excluded, and the usual 1,000-row table limit applies.

  • It counts your links, not your name. By Google's definition, an AI Overview that recommends your brand but links only to a review site records no impression for you.
  • It has no position or prominence metric. A link at the top of an answer and a link further down each count as an impression once shown, so the report can't tell you whether you were the recommendation or a supporting source.
  • An empty report can be a setting. Search Console's Search generative AI control, rolled out to all websites on Aug. 31, 2026, lets a property exclude itself from AI Overviews, AI Mode, and AI features in Discover. Including is the default; an excluded site gets no impressions or traffic from those features.

The standard Performance report still blends AI into Web

Clicks from AI features are in Search Console, just not separately. Google's AI features documentation says sites appearing in AI Overviews and AI Mode are "included in the overall search traffic in Search Console," reported in the Performance report within the Web search type. Per Google's counting rules, a click on a link in an AI Overview or in AI Mode counts as a click, all links in an AI Overview share the single position the Overview occupies, and a follow-up question in AI Mode counts as a new query.

The result: your Web clicks include AI clicks, and no filter separates them. The workable approach is comparative. Track pages with rising AI impressions against pages without them over the same window, and treat the difference as a correlation, not an attribution.

Bing Webmaster Tools: AI Performance

Microsoft launched AI Performance in public preview on Feb. 10, 2026. It "shows how publisher content appears across Microsoft Copilot, AI-generated summaries in Bing, and select partner integrations." The dashboard reports total citations, average cited pages per day, citation counts per URL, a trend line, and grounding queries, "the key phrases the AI used when retrieving content." Microsoft notes that the grounding-query data "represents a sample of overall citation activity."

On June 16, 2026, Microsoft added 4 preview features: Intents and Topics, which classify and cluster grounding queries; Compare, which overlays a previous period; and Citation Share, "the percentage of citations attributed to your site out of all citations shown across all sites for that same grounding query." Microsoft is explicit about the limit: Citation Share "does not expose competitor domains, represent traffic share, or assign quality scores to content." No click or traffic metric appears among the ones Microsoft lists for the report.

Grounding queries make it worth opening even if Copilot isn't a priority: they show the searches the AI ran to retrieve your pages, which Search Console's AI report doesn't expose. For Copilot itself, it is the only free first-party view of citations. SolCrys doesn't measure Copilot, so this is the report we recommend for it. Microsoft doesn't name the partner integrations, so don't read it as a proxy for ChatGPT.

GA4: the AI Assistant channel

GA4 added an AI Assistant default channel on May 13, 2026. When a session's referrer matches a recognized AI assistant, GA4 sets the medium to ai-assistant and the campaign to (ai-assistant), and the session lands in the new channel. Google's release note names ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude; the channel definition names ChatGPT, Gemini, DeepSeek, Copilot, and Grok. The help page doesn't publish the full list.

Four kinds of AI-influenced visits fall outside it:

  • AI Overviews and AI Mode. GA4 files them under Organic Search, which it defines as "including Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode."
  • Visits without a referrer. The channel depends on the referrer. A buyer who copies your URL out of an answer, or types your name into the address bar later, arrives as Direct.
  • Assistants GA4 doesn't recognize. They fall to other channel rules, usually Referral. Perplexity isn't named on either Google page, so check your Referral report for perplexity.ai before assuming it's covered.
  • Everyone who read the answer and didn't click. GA4 counts arrivals, so influence without a visit never appears.

Two free cross-checks

ChatGPT tags its outbound links: OpenAI's publisher FAQ says ChatGPT "automatically includes the UTM parameter utm_source=chatgpt.com in referral URLs," so filter session source for chatgpt.com as well. And Microsoft Clarity, also free, has its own AIPlatform and PaidAIPlatform channels, which list Claude, Copilot, Gemini, ChatGPT, and Perplexity, count only visits from their standalone sites, and carry the same caveat Microsoft states plainly: "If the source is hidden, the traffic might appear as Direct."

Expect small numbers either way; what AEO does to your web traffic covers why referral counts understate AI influence.

Merchant Center: AI performance insights

For retailers, this is the only one of the 4 reports that compares you with a competitor set. Google made AI performance insights generally available on Sept. 16, 2026. Per the Merchant Center help page, it covers English-language queries for accounts in Australia, Canada, India, New Zealand, and the US, and lives under Analytics, then Products, then the AI performance tab.

The headline metric is your share of voice for conversational queries with shopping intent in AI Mode and AI Overviews: your AI impressions divided by the total across you and your competitor set. The report also splits queries into 3 shopping stages (Discovery, Evaluation, and Ready to buy) and lists the top terms shoppers use and popular attributes that may be missing from your product data.

  • Organic only. "Paid Ads traffic isn't included in this report."
  • One product category at a time. There is no report across all categories.
  • A competitor set you don't pick. "You can't change your competitors"; if none are defined, share of voice shows 100%.
  • A few days behind. Data updates daily "with a few days lag."
  • Products on Google only. It doesn't cover services, non-Google assistants, or what the answer said about a product.

What none of them do

Put the 4 reports side by side and the same 5 gaps remain.

  • The answer itself. No report shows the text of an AI answer, so you can't see how you were described.
  • Recommended versus mentioned. An impression or a citation says your page was shown or used. It doesn't say whether the answer told the buyer to choose you or a competitor; see AI Recommendation Score for why that gap matters.
  • Accuracy. No report says whether a claim about your pricing, integrations, or capabilities was right; see is AI telling the truth about your brand.
  • Mentions carried by other people's pages. When an answer names you through a review site or a comparison post, Search Console and Bing record nothing for you.
  • Engines outside the platform. ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Claude answers reach your reports only as visits, and only when someone clicks with a referrer intact.

The trade-off that runs the other way

The platform reports measure real users; answer-side tracking measures a fixed, sampled set of prompts. Each is strong where the other is weak. A fixed prompt set is what lets you test a change: same questions, same engines, before and after. Real-user reports are what tell you whether the pages you changed gained impressions, citations, or visits. You need both halves.

How to run them together

An afternoon of setup covers all 4, and the join key is the page URL.

  • Search Console: open the generative AI performance report, confirm the Search generative AI control is set to include, and export impressions by page each month, since the 1,000-row table limit makes a regular export worth it.
  • Bing Webmaster Tools: verify the site, open AI Performance, and export page-level citations and grounding queries; use Compare for period-over-period.
  • GA4: open the AI Assistant channel, scan Referral for AI domains GA4 didn't recognize, and filter session source for chatgpt.com.
  • Merchant Center (retail only): open the AI performance tab for each priority product category.
  • Join on the URL: keep one sheet with one row per page: Search Console AI impressions, Bing citations, GA4 AI Assistant sessions by landing page, and the URLs answer engines cite on your buyer prompts. Pages cited everywhere but rarely visited are your click gap.
  • Start the baseline now. These reports launched between February and September 2026, so every trend is short. Movement is only readable against a baseline you started before the change.

What the joined view shows

Illustrative scenario only. A data-warehouse vendor's pricing-comparison page shows steady AI impressions in Search Console, a few dozen Copilot citations a month in Bing, and a trickle of AI Assistant sessions in GA4. Every free report says the page is present. Answer-side tracking on 20 buyer prompts shows why the sessions stay low: ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews cite the page, then recommend 2 larger competitors first and describe the vendor with a pricing model it retired last year. The fix is on the page and on the third-party sources the engines trust, and the free reports are how you watch whether impressions and visits follow.

FAQ

Does Search Console show clicks from AI Overviews or AI Mode?

Not separately. The generative AI performance report shows impressions only, with no clicks, CTR, or queries. Clicks on links in AI Overviews and AI Mode are counted in the standard Performance report under the Web search type, blended with every other web click, and no filter separates them.

What does the Search Console generative AI performance report show?

How many times links to your site were shown in Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, grouped by page, country, date, or device. Google announced it on June 3, 2026, and says it reached all websites worldwide on Aug. 31, 2026. It counts your links, not your brand name, so an answer that names you while linking only to a review site doesn't register.

How do I see ChatGPT traffic in GA4?

Open the AI Assistant default channel, added May 13, 2026, which groups sessions whose referrer matches a recognized AI assistant, including ChatGPT. OpenAI says ChatGPT adds utm_source=chatgpt.com to its referral links, so filter session source for chatgpt.com as a cross-check. Visits without a referrer land in Direct, and AI Overviews and AI Mode stay in Organic Search, so treat the channel as a floor.

What is Bing Webmaster Tools AI Performance?

A free Microsoft report, in public preview since Feb. 10, 2026, that shows how often your pages are cited in Microsoft Copilot, AI-generated summaries in Bing, and select partner integrations, plus the grounding queries behind those citations. Citation Share, added June 16, 2026, shows your share of all citations for a grounding query without naming competitors. It has no click or traffic metric, and it is the free report to use for Copilot.

Does Merchant Center show how my products appear in AI answers?

Yes, on Google's surfaces. AI performance insights, generally available since Sept. 16, 2026, shows your share of voice in AI Mode and AI Overviews against a competitor set Merchant Center defines, for English-language queries in Australia, Canada, India, New Zealand, and the US. It covers organic results only and one product category at a time.

Do I still need an AEO tool if Google and Bing report AI for free?

Use the free reports first; they are the only first-party data on these surfaces. Whether you need more depends on the questions you have to answer. None of them shows what an answer said, whether it recommended you or a competitor, whether it was accurate, or what ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Claude said to buyers who didn't click. If those questions matter to your pipeline, you need answer-side measurement on a fixed prompt set; if they don't yet, the free reports are enough.

Why is my Search Console generative AI report empty?

Google's help page points to 3 possibilities: your property may not have access yet, your site may not have enough impressions in AI features, or the site may be excluded through the Search generative AI control in Search Console's settings. Including is the default, and an excluded site receives no impressions or traffic from those features.

Does SolCrys connect to GA4 or Search Console?

No. SolCrys has no GA4 or Search Console connector and doesn't read your analytics. It measures the answer side (mentions, cited URLs, Recommendation Score, and Answer Accuracy), and you join that to Search Console, Bing, and GA4 data by the cited URL in your own analysis. SolCrys also doesn't measure Copilot; Bing Webmaster Tools is the free report for it.

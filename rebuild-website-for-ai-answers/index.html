Should you rebuild your website for AI answers? Replace, extend or hybrid

Website platforms now ship most AEO basics, and some suggest you move to get the rest. Here are the three ways a marketing team can respond, when each one is right, and how SolCrys supports all three.

By Eason Wang, Co-Founder & CPO, SolCrys

Updated 2026-11-19

Questions this guide answers

  • Do I need to rebuild my website to show up in AI answers?
  • Should I switch website platforms for AEO?
  • Do I need an AEO plugin for my CMS?
  • What is a hybrid approach to AEO?
  • How do I prove a website change improved AI answers?

Direct answer

Keep the website platform you have, unless you would replace it anyway. AI engines read pages, not platforms. Google says AI Overviews have no additional requirements beyond a page being indexed and eligible for a snippet, and ChatGPT search needs its crawler allowed. What moves an answer is what the pages behind it say, and the proof that it moved is the same buyer questions, asked on each engine before and after the change.

That leaves three paths, and we recommend the smallest one that closes the gap: extend the platform you have, go hybrid when a few key pages need rebuilding, and replace the site only when it fails you for reasons of its own. SolCrys supports all three: SolCrys AEO when your team does the work, and SolCrys Sites when we do.

Why the question comes up now

Publishing stopped being the hard part. Website platforms and CMS plugins generate structured data and metadata, manage crawler access and draft pages on request. Some AEO tools write drafts straight into a CMS, and website platforms have started selling AEO features of their own; our comparison of CMS integrations maps who does what. The pitch that follows is one of two: move to the platform that has AEO built in, or add a connector to the one you have.

Both pitches treat AEO as a property of the software. The engines don't. Google says SEO best practices remain relevant to its AI features, and neither Google nor OpenAI asks which CMS served the page.

Replace: right for its own reasons

A migration changes the container. Unless the old site kept engines from reaching or reading its pages, for example by blocking their crawlers or rendering its text only in JavaScript, a new site with the same copy gives them nothing new to read, and the answers have no reason to change. A rebuild that does rewrite the pages can move answers, but then the rewrite did the work, and it would have worked without the move.

A move also carries a cost that AEO pitches leave out. Google says moving most pages of a small or medium site can take a few weeks, and Search visibility may fluctuate meanwhile.

If you are replacing the site anyway, the move is also your best chance to fix the copy, and it should launch with a measurement on either side of it. That is how SolCrys Sites runs a rebuild, keeping your pages at the same addresses and carrying your existing redirects across. A rebuild is never our answer to an AI search problem that better pages would solve.

Before you replace a platform for AI search, four questions settle it.

  • Would we replace it if AI search didn't exist? If not, the platform isn't your problem.
  • Do our pages say what the product is, who it's for and the proof behind each claim? If not, start with the pages.
  • Do we have a baseline on fixed questions, per engine? If not, take it before anything moves; afterward it's too late.
  • Is every URL that AI answers cite today mapped to its new address? If not, add a permanent redirect for each one before launch.

Extend: your platform, your team, better tools

Structured data, clean metadata and sensible crawler rules are table stakes; if your platform doesn't keep them consistent across hundreds of pages, a plugin earns its place. But markup can only restate the page: Google's structured data guidelines say it must be a true representation of the page content. If the page doesn't state a fact, schema can't legitimately add it, so the fixes that move answers are mostly changes to what pages say, made by people who know the product.

One loop under all three

The path decides who does the work. It doesn't change the loop, Measure → Diagnose → Execute → Verify, and the same four rules apply to every path.

  • Fix the questions first. Choose them from buyer intents and the persona asking, and run them on each engine you track (SolCrys covers ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Gemini, Perplexity and Claude) before anyone writes, so the result can't be picked after the fact. The method is in our Golden Prompt Set guide.
  • Trace each gap to where its fix lives. Some gaps are on your pages, some on third-party sources the engines cite, and some are a crawler setting. Any of the three paths can fix the first and third kinds on your site; the second needs work off your site. The work plan guide shows how to sort them.
  • Write fixes from approved facts. Every change starts from your Corporate Context, the record of approved facts, claims and proof, so the website says what your sales deck and demo say. The change goes into the real page that people read, not a separate version for bots.
  • Ask the same questions again. Once a page ships, its URL is recorded and the same questions keep running on each engine you track, so you can see when each one first cites it. Read the result over a window of runs, and check whether questions you didn't touch moved too.

FAQ

How long does a page change take to show up in AI answers?

It varies by engine and by how soon each one recrawls the page. The Cornelis and NextSilicon results were measured four weeks and 45 days after their baselines, and those windows include the writing time.

Do you guarantee AI engines will cite the changed pages?

No. We commit to the questions and the remeasurement, and we report exactly what moved. The engines decide which sources they name.

AEO vs SEO: what changes when answers replace result pages

SEO and AEO are one system, not rivals. SEO and authority still decide whether a page is retrieved at all; AEO decides whether it is cited and described accurately once it is. How much SEO carries over depends on the engine: Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode inherit Search ranking, so SEO is most of the work there, while pure-RAG engines like ChatGPT and Perplexity cite sources that can diverge sharply from your Google rank. What stays the same, what changes, and the metrics for each.

Direct answer

SEO helps a page rank in search results. AEO helps that page become a source AI engines retrieve, cite, and describe accurately inside generated answers. They are one system, not a replacement: SEO and authority still gate whether you are retrieved at all, and AEO decides whether you are the source the model quotes once you are.

How much SEO carries over depends on the engine

Treating AEO as one rule across every engine is the most common mistake. How tightly it depends on SEO splits by surface:

  • Google AI Overviews and AI Mode inherit Google's Search index, core ranking systems, and query fan-out, so strong SEO is most of the work. On Google's surfaces, AEO is an operating layer on top of SEO, not a separate channel (per Google's 2026 AI optimization guidance).
  • ChatGPT Search, Perplexity, and Claude run true retrieval over their own reading lists, so citation can diverge sharply from your Google rank. A page that ranks nowhere can still be the cited source, and a top-ranking page can be ignored.

The divergence is measurable

How far citation drifts from ranking is not a hunch. Only about 12% of AI-cited URLs appear in Google's top ten organic results for the same query, and ChatGPT and Perplexity overlap on only about 11% of cited sources because each reads a different slice of the web. Ranking is necessary on Google's AI surfaces and far from sufficient on the pure-RAG ones.

We see the same shape in our own category. Over a recent seven-day window, AI answers about AEO tools pulled from 13,739 citations across 1,534 distinct domains — a consensus far wider than any single results page, with no domain above about 6.4%. Our own site is cited across all five engines we track but sits in the top 10 of those domains, just under 2% of citations: a solid SEO-style position that still leaves most of the category's citations with third-party sources. Being retrievable is the floor; being corroborated across the sources each engine reads is what wins the citation. (SolCrys measurement, 7-day window ending 2026-06-18.)

Why SEO remains the foundation

AI answer engines still depend on discoverable, crawlable, authoritative web content. If a page is blocked, thin, slow, or ambiguous, it is less likely to become a useful source for generated answers.

  • Technical SEO makes pages discoverable.
  • Content depth gives systems usable evidence.
  • Internal links clarify topical relationships.
  • Freshness reduces the chance of outdated answers.

FAQ

Does AEO replace SEO?

No. AEO is an operating layer on top of SEO, not a replacement. SEO and authority still decide whether a page is crawlable and retrievable, which is a precondition for being cited. On Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, SEO does most of the work because those surfaces inherit Search ranking. On pure-RAG engines like ChatGPT and Perplexity, you also need to be the clearest, most corroborated source once retrieved.

Do Google AI Overviews use my SEO rankings?

Yes. AI Overviews and AI Mode are built on Google's Search index, core ranking systems, and query fan-out, so your existing SEO strength is the main driver of whether you appear there. That is different from ChatGPT or Perplexity, which retrieve over their own reading lists, so a strong Google rank does not guarantee a citation on those engines.

Should a brand create separate AEO pages?

Only when the page answers a real question better than an existing page. AEO rewards clarity and usefulness, not duplicated pages with new labels.

Do AI answer engines use keywords?

They use queries and retrieval systems, but matching is more semantic. Use the words customers use, then answer the underlying intent completely.

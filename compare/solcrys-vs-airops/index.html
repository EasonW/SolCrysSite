SolCrys vs AirOps: an honest comparison from a team building in the same category

AirOps positions itself as a growth platform for AI search and Google with a content-execution workflow that includes Quill (their AI agent captain), Insights, Action, and Brand Kit. We built SolCrys as a PLG-first AEO workflow product anchored on Corporate Context governance and an answer-gap-to-action-brief-to-re-test loop. The honest choice turns on whether your primary need is scaling on-brand content production, or finding AI answer gaps and proving the fix moved the answer.

Direct answer

AirOps and SolCrys both move past the dashboard-only model and ship workflow products. AirOps's center of gravity is content creation at scale, with Quill as the AI agent captain, Brand Kit governing voice, and 10+ CMS integrations for direct publishing. Our center of gravity is the answer-gap-to-action-brief-to-re-test loop, with Corporate Context governing approved brand facts and claims as the grounding source for actions. Pick AirOps if scaling content production is your top job; pick SolCrys if proving fixes moved AI answers is.

Disclosure: we build SolCrys, so this is opinionated. We use AirOps's own product pages as the source of truth for what AirOps does and we tell you explicitly when we would pick AirOps over SolCrys.

Scope of this comparison: this is a positioning-level review drawn from each platform's public marketing surface - homepages, product pages, pricing pages. We have not run both products side-by-side on the same workspace, so the comparison is between published positioning and verifiable feature claims, not between practitioner-validated outcomes. Treat it as buyer orientation, not a tested product review.

What AirOps does, in their own words

AirOps describes itself as a growth platform for AI search and Google. We summarize each module from airops.com so the comparison is grounded in their own positioning, not ours.

  • Quill: described as your AI agent captain, the orchestration layer that drives content workflows.
  • Insights: visibility dashboards and citation tracking across AI search platforms, with multi-engine coverage on Pro and above.
  • Action: content creation and execution tools that turn opportunities into published assets.
  • Brand Kit: a governance layer for consistent brand voice at scale.
  • Workflows + Grids: repeatable process chains and data organization features.
  • Offsite: third-party mention discovery and measurement.
  • AirOps MCPs: a Claude integration for workflow accessibility.

What SolCrys is, in our own words

SolCrys is an AEO workflow product organized around four published platform layers (see our Answer Engine Optimization page for the long version).

  • AI Visibility Measurement - mentions, citations, share of recommendation, competitors, sentiment, and answer accuracy at the prompt level across plan-included engines, with daily monitoring on priority prompts and rolling 7/30-day windows.
  • Corporate Context Layer - approved brand facts, product claims, proof, voice, competitor framing, and guardrails as the governed source for every AEO action.
  • AEO Agent - today SolCrys turns answer gaps into Deep Analyses, content briefs, comparison sections, FAQ improvements, and source recommendations. Publish-ready drafting and agent-assisted CMS shipping are being built.
  • Action-to-Result Tracking - after an action ships, we re-run the same prompt set across the same engines and report movement on citation rate, answer accuracy, and recommendation share.

Where we agree with AirOps

The places we agree shape the rest of the comparison.

  • AI search needs a workflow product, not just a dashboard - both of us ship the loop side rather than stop at the report.
  • Brand governance matters when agents are involved - AirOps's Brand Kit and our Corporate Context are responses to the same risk of agents going off-brand.
  • Multi-engine coverage is table stakes - one engine is not a workflow.
  • Citations and source tracking are first-class objects, not a footnote on a mention.

Where we made different bets

These are the choices that explain who each product is for.

Content-execution-first vs answer-gap-first

AirOps's product gravitates to content creation as the primary surface: Quill the AI agent captain, Action for content execution, large task budgets (the Pro tier publishes 75,000 tasks/mo for content production per their pricing page), and CMS integrations for shipping. We built SolCrys around the answer-gap loop - the action is the consequence of a detected gap on a tracked prompt, not the starting point. Both shapes are valid; they imply different team rhythms.

Brand Kit vs Corporate Context

AirOps's Brand Kit governs voice and templates so that content scaled through Quill stays on-brand. Our Corporate Context layer governs approved brand facts, product claims, proof, and competitor framing - it is a factual grounding source for what agents say about the brand, not just a style guide. If you need agents to never publish an off-brand sentence, Brand Kit is the right shape; if you need agents to never publish an off-fact sentence, Corporate Context is.

CMS publishing depth

AirOps's pricing page lists 10+ CMS integrations and includes CMS, SEO, AEO, Social, and Project integrations on Pro and above - direct publishing is part of the offer today. We do not have that integration breadth, and we are explicit about it: today SolCrys delivers Deep Analyses and structured briefs your team or agency ships through your CMS. If end-to-end agent-shipping inside one platform is a primary buying criterion, AirOps is the more mature pick.

When you should pick AirOps (we mean this)

If your team meets several of the criteria below, we would rather you buy what fits your job than buy us.

  • Scaling on-brand content production is your top job, and you want a workflow with templates, tasks, and CMS publishing built in.
  • You want a $0 starting tier and the option to scale into a custom enterprise contract within the same product.
  • Direct CMS publishing across 10+ integrations is a primary buying criterion.
  • Your team is content-led and wants an AI agent captain (Quill) as the orchestration surface.

When you should pick SolCrys

And these are the buying signals we built SolCrys for.

  • Your top job is finding AI answer gaps and proving the fix moved them - measurement, diagnosis, briefs, and re-test as a weekly loop.
  • You need Corporate Context as a factual grounding layer for what agents say about your brand, not just a brand-voice style guide.
  • You are an agency packaging AEO as a service line - our Agency $1,499 and Agency Pro $4,999 are built around client workspaces and white-label reporting.
  • You sell on retail (Amazon Rufus, Walmart Sparky, ChatGPT Shopping) and want a scoped Retail AI add-on rather than a separate procurement track.

FAQ

Is AirOps cheaper than SolCrys?

AirOps's Insights tier starts at $0/mo; our Starter is $29/mo. The free-vs-paid framing is misleading though - AirOps's free Insights tier targets individuals using AI templates and content production at limited capacity, while our Starter buys you a 20-prompt monitoring set with Deep Analyses, Content Audits, and MCP read access. Compare what each $0-$29 entry actually does, not just the headline price.

Does AirOps have something like Corporate Context?

AirOps's Brand Kit governs voice and templates for content at scale. Corporate Context governs approved brand facts, claims, proof, voice, and competitor framing as the grounding source for actions. Different scopes - one is a style guide, the other is a factual source of truth. If you need both, you can run both, but most teams choose based on which failure mode they fear more: off-voice or off-fact.

Should we use both?

Usually no. If you are already deep on AirOps for content creation, the cleanest stack is to add SolCrys for the answer-gap workflow without rebuilding measurement. If you are deep on SolCrys and need scaled content production, you can layer AirOps for content workflow without duplicating measurement. Two of the same shape is not a recommended pattern.

Why is this comparison published by SolCrys?

Because pretending we are neutral would be worse. Buyer guides written by category participants shape the buyer's mental model whether or not we participate, and we would rather you have our honest take next to AirOps's product pages than have to assemble it from scratch. We do not quote AirOps's blog or research as authority - only AirOps's own product pages. If you find a factual error here, tell us and we will correct it.

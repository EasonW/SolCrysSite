Free AEO tools, compared by the loop: which ones fix, not just score you (2026)

Direct answer

There are plenty of free ways to find out how visible your brand is in AI answers in 2026. There is almost no free way to do anything about it. That gap is what this guide is about.

Sort the free options by the job and they fall into a four-step loop: Measure (where do I stand in AI answers?) → Diagnose (what specifically is wrong?) → Execute (give me the exact fix to ship) → Verify (re-test the same prompts to prove the fix worked). We surveyed 16 free offerings against those four steps. The result is lopsided: every free tool measures, some diagnose, and — with one honest exception we name below — none of them executes. They hand you a score and a gap list and stop.

The one distinction that decides everything: a score is not a fix

Most "free AEO tool" lists rank tools by how good the number is — how many engines, how fresh, how pretty the dashboard. That is the wrong axis, because almost all of them live at the same step of the loop. They are scoreboards. A scoreboard tells you that you are losing 1–0. It does not tell you to swap your striker.

The expensive, skilled, slow part of AEO is never the measurement. It is the work after: figuring out the specific change that will move a specific answer, writing it, shipping it, and proving it moved. So the only distinction that changes your week is whether the free tool does any of that work for you, or hands the score back and wishes you luck.

That single question — score, or fix? — is what the comparison below sorts on.

"But Writesonic's free LLM Optimizer rewrites my text" — the one honest counter, handled

It does, and it is worth naming so this page is trustworthy rather than convenient. Writesonic ships two free standalone tools: an AI Search Grader (a score) and an LLM Optimizer that will rewrite a snippet to add quotes and citations. The rewrite is real "execution."

But it is not loop execution, and the difference is the whole point. The LLM Optimizer is a disconnected text box. It does not know which buyer prompt you are losing, it is not fed by an audit of your live page, it does not emit the JSON-LD a crawler needs, and nothing ties the rewrite back to a re-test of the prompt set that scored you. Writesonic does close the full loop — measure, diagnose, execute, verify — but only on its paid GEO tiers (from ~$199/mo), not in the free tools. So on a free basis it lands the same place as everyone else: a score on one screen, a generic rewriter on another, and a manual leap in between.

That is the bar this comparison holds: does the free tool execute the fix that its own measurement told you to make? By that bar, the count is one.

Why the whole free category stops at the scoreboard

This is not an accident of any one vendor; it is the business model. A free AI-visibility score is the most efficient lead magnet in this category — it is cheap to run, it produces an alarming number ("you appear in 4% of answers"), and the alarm is what sells the paid plan. The recommendation engine, the content drafting, the schema generation, the re-test — the parts that cost real compute and reputation — are exactly what the paid tier is for. So the free experience is engineered to stop precisely at the moment you would want to act.

That logic is why "free AEO tool" so reliably means "free AEO grader." It is also why the one place a free tier crosses into Execute is worth a closer look.

Is there a free AEO tool that fixes my content, not just scores it?

No — not on a free basis, including SolCrys.

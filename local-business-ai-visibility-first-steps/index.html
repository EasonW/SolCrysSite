What a local business should do first to show up in ChatGPT, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews

Local business owners keep asking which actions most improve their visibility in ChatGPT, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews. The order matters more than any single tactic. First, see what the answers say about you today, city by city and engine by engine. Then fix your business facts on the platforms each engine reads: Google Business Profile for Google's AI, Yelp and Bing Places for ChatGPT and Copilot, Apple Business for Apple Maps and Siri. Then make your website state your services, prices, service areas, and hours plainly, get onto the third-party pages the answers already cite, and build reviews steadily within each platform's rules. Recheck the same questions monthly. This checklist gives each step with what the platforms themselves say, as of Oct. 14, 2026.

Direct answer

Do the work in this order. Each step makes the next one count.

1. See what the answers say now. Ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Google, and Perplexity 8 to 10 questions the way your customers would, with your city in them, and note who gets named and which sites get cited.

2. Fix your facts where each engine reads them. Google's AI reads your Google Business Profile. ChatGPT draws local listings from partners: Yelp says its ratings and reviews now power ChatGPT's local results in relevant categories, and Microsoft's Bing is one of ChatGPT's search providers. Apple Maps and Siri read Apple Business. Keep your name, address, phone, and hours identical on all of them.

3. Say it plainly on your website: each service, typical prices, the areas you serve by name, and your hours.

4. Get onto the pages the answers already cite: the directories, local best-of lists, and local press from step 1.

5. Build reviews steadily, within each platform's rules.

6. Recheck the same questions monthly. Changes take weeks to show, and one answer is not a trend.

No engine reads one shared local index. Each one assembles local answers from its own sources, which is why a competitor can be named on one engine and missing from another. Step 1: See what the answers say now

Set aside an afternoon. Write 8 to 10 questions in your customers' words, with your city or neighborhood in each one: "best family dentist in Bellevue," "emergency plumber open Sunday in Tacoma," "quiet sushi place for a work dinner near Pike Place." Add 2 questions that check your own facts, such as your hours or whether you take a certain insurance.

Ask each question on ChatGPT, Gemini, Google (AI Overviews or AI Mode), and Perplexity. Answers change from one run to the next, so ask each one at least twice. For each answer, record:

  • Were you named, and where in the list?
  • Who was named instead? Those are the businesses the engine trusts for this question.
  • Which sites were cited? These become your list for step 4.
  • Was anything wrong? Old hours, a closed location, a service you don't offer. Fix these first in step 2.

Step 2: Fix your facts where each engine reads them

Google says there are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and its AI optimization guide (updated July 10, 2026) says Business Profiles "can help your products and services to be visible in both AI responses and other Google Search results." The same logic applies to every engine: it can only repeat the facts it can find. Do this in the first week.

  • Google Business Profile: primary and secondary categories, every service, hours including holiday hours, attributes, photos, and a description in plain language. Ask Maps now lets customers suggest edits, such as new hours read from a photo of your door (Google), so review suggested edits regularly.
  • Yelp: claim the listing and complete categories, hours, services, and photos. It feeds ChatGPT and Perplexity.
  • Bing Places (now at bing.com/forbusiness): claim and complete it. It feeds Copilot, and Bing is one of ChatGPT's search providers.
  • Apple Business: claim your place card for Apple Maps and Siri.
  • Your vertical's directories: the ones that showed up in step 1, such as a legal, medical, or travel directory.
  • One version of the truth: the same business name, address, phone number, and hours on every listing and on your website.
  • Don't block the crawlers. OpenAI says sites that opt out of its search crawler, OAI-SearchBot, "will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers" (OpenAI). In Search Console, check that the Search generative AI control is set to include, which is the default.

Step 3: Say it plainly on your website

Your website owns your facts and converts the visit once an engine names you. Make it easy to quote. Do this in weeks 1 and 2.

  • A page for each main service: what it includes, who it's for, and a typical price or price range.
  • The areas you serve, by name: neighborhoods and nearby towns, listed on the pages where they apply.
  • Hours, holiday hours, and how to book, in text, not only in an image.
  • Credentials: licenses, certifications, insurance accepted, years in business.
  • The questions customers ask you, answered in their words on the relevant page.
  • Structured data that matches your listings. Google says structured data "isn't required for generative AI search," so treat LocalBusiness markup as a way to state your name, address, and hours unambiguously, not as a shortcut. The companion guide shows the most useful types.
  • No pages for cities you don't serve. Google's spam policies list "having multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" as doorway abuse (Google).

Step 4: Get onto the pages the answers already cite

Go back to the sites you recorded in step 1. For local recommendations, they are usually third-party pages: category directories, review sites, "best of" lists from local publications, and local news. Sort them into 3 groups and work through them in weeks 2 to 4.

Cited pageWhat to do
A directory or review site that lists your competitorsClaim or create your listing and complete it with the same facts as step 2
A local "best of" list or guideSend the editor accurate facts and a reason you belong on it; never pay for placement without disclosure
Local news or community pagesOffer something worth covering: an event, a sponsorship, local data, or expert comment

Why step 4 has to be earned

Google's AI optimization guide warns against "seeking inauthentic 'mentions'" across the web, and its spam policies now cover attempts to manipulate generative AI answers (which tactics cross the line). A listing you earn holds up; a placement you buy quietly can cost you the domain.

Step 5: Build reviews steadily, within the rules

Reviews feed the local data engines read. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help a business's local ranking, its AI review summaries are each "based on reviews from the past year," and Ask Maps draws on reviews from Maps contributors (Google). Yelp says its ratings and reviews now power ChatGPT's local results in relevant categories. Because the summaries look back one year, a steady flow of recent reviews matters more than an old pile.

The rules differ by platform, and breaking them costs more than the reviews are worth.

  • Google: asking is fine. Offering anything in return is not: Google's content policy prohibits incentives "such as payment, discounts, free goods and/or services," and businesses may not "discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews." Ask every customer, not only the happy ones.
  • Yelp: stricter. Yelp tells businesses not to ask for reviews at all (Search Engine Land). Let customers know you're on Yelp, and leave it there.
  • Every platform: reply to reviews, especially negative ones, with specifics. A reply that fixes a problem is evidence too.
  • For why the text of reviews matters as much as the star rating, see why AI ignores your star rating.

Step 6: Recheck the same questions monthly

Ask the same questions from step 1, on the same engines, with the same city, once a month. Changes to listings and third-party pages take weeks to reach answers, so judge a change over 2 or 3 checks, not one. The engines and their data partners keep changing too: several of the sources in this guide, including Yelp's ChatGPT deal and Ask Maps, are from 2026.

What to skip

These look like shortcuts and either don't work or put the business at risk.

  • Buying reviews, offering incentives, or filtering who gets asked. All three break Google's rules.
  • Pages for every nearby city you don't actually serve. Google calls this doorway abuse.
  • Paying for a spot on a "best of" list without disclosure.
  • An llms.txt file as a local fix. No engine has said it uses one for local answers (why llms.txt is not a strategy).
  • Special "AI schema." There isn't any; Google says structured data isn't required for its AI features.

FAQ

What is the first thing a local business should do to show up in AI answers?

Check what the answers say now. Ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Google, and Perplexity 8 to 10 questions the way your customers would, with your city in them, at least twice each. Note who gets named, which sites get cited, and any wrong facts. That tells you which listings to fix and which sites to get onto.

Where does ChatGPT get local business information?

From web search through partners, including Microsoft, and from local data partners. OpenAI says ChatGPT may share a user's location with trusted third-party providers for nearby business listings. Yelp says its ratings and reviews began powering ChatGPT's local experience in relevant categories in 2026, and ChatGPT can find reservations through OpenTable, Resy, and Yelp.

Does Google Business Profile affect AI Overviews?

Yes. Google says Business Profiles can help your products and services be visible in AI responses as well as other Search results, and its AI Mode shows ratings, reviews, and opening hours from its local business data. Keep the profile complete and accurate, and review the edits customers suggest.

Should I ask customers for reviews?

On Google, yes, as long as you ask every customer and offer nothing in return; incentives and asking only happy customers both break Google's rules. Yelp tells businesses not to ask for reviews at all.

How long does it take to show up in ChatGPT or Google's AI answers?

Nobody publishes a timeline, and it varies by engine and by fix. Correcting your own listings is the fastest part of the work; earning a place on the third-party pages engines cite takes longer. Recheck the same questions monthly and judge a change over 2 or 3 checks rather than one answer.

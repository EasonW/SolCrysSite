Direct answer

A source-layer strategy is the deliberate plan for which domains and content surfaces AI engines retrieve when they answer a question about your brand. It has three layers: owned (your .com, docs, blog), earned (editorial mentions, analyst coverage, podcast appearances), and community (Reddit, G2/Capterra, YouTube, forums). Most citations point away from any brand's own site (85.7% went to third-party domains in a 2026 study of 167,551 citations about 128 brands), and which third parties get cited now varies by engine, so you need all three layers, sequenced and measured per engine. Owned pages are the destination; earned and community sources are where most of the evidence an engine assembles actually lives.

What is a source layer?

The source layer is the set of domains and content surfaces that AI engines actually retrieve from when constructing answers about you. It is the AI-search equivalent of the off-page authority graph in traditional SEO, but the inputs and the ranking signals are different, and the population of cited sources is broader than backlinks ever were.

Each engine builds that list differently. ChatGPT Search draws on third-party search providers (OpenAI's help center links Microsoft's privacy statement), OpenAI's crawlers (OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User), and on-demand page fetches. Perplexity runs real-time retrieval across the open web. Google AI Overviews and AI Mode draw on Google's Search index, core ranking systems, and query fan-out. Anthropic doesn't name Claude's search provider (Brave Search was reported in 2025 and still appears on its subprocessor list). Gemini is grounded in Google Search. In every case the answer is sourced from a finite, observable list of URLs. That list is your source layer. You can measure it. You can change it. You can lose it.

We separate it into three layers because the work to influence each layer is fundamentally different: different specialists, different budgets, different timelines, different ethical guardrails.

A real source-layer strategy treats all three as a portfolio, with explicit goals and explicit non-goals per layer.

  • Owned: content you publish on domains you control. Your .com, your product documentation, your blog, your help center, your changelog, your customer-story library.
  • Earned: third-party editorial coverage on independent domains. Trade press (TechRadar, TechCrunch, vertical industry publications), analyst reports (Forrester, Gartner, IDC, vertical analysts), guest essays on partner blogs, podcast appearances where you're the guest.
  • Community: user-generated and community-platform content. Reddit threads, G2/Capterra/TrustRadius reviews, YouTube videos and shorts, Quora and Stack Exchange answers, niche forums, LinkedIn long-form posts.

Implication 1: owned is a minority of citations, but it has a job

On questions about a specific brand, 14.3% of citations in the Zatuchin study went to the brand's own site. On category-wide buyer prompts in our study, no single vendor's domain took more than about 2.4%, which is a strong result for one domain in a field of thousands. Owned pages are what engines quote for your own facts (pricing, specs, methodology) and where the referral click lands. Build them well; don't expect them to carry the category alone.

Implication 2: the third-party mix is engine-specific and can move in a week

In August 2026 ChatGPT all but stopped citing Reddit (from 3.83% to 0.52% of citations on one tracker's data, as reported by Search Engine Journal), while Google AI Overviews kept citing it and Claude never has. A plan that assumes one static source mix across engines is already out of date, so measure per engine. If you've never measured your own distribution, that's step one: a citation gap audit returns your starting numbers per layer for a defined prompt set, with URL-level evidence.

The 3-layer source strategy

Below is the playbook per layer: the goal, what it takes, and the specific plays. Treat it as a menu; almost no brand should run every play in year one.

Owned layer

Goal: own the answer for the prompts where engines do prefer first-party sources, usually deep product questions, pricing, methodology, security and compliance pages, and "what is your X" definitional queries.

Cost / time / specialist: in-house content team, or one senior writer plus one editor. $0 in net new tooling if you already have a CMS. Timeline: 90 days for compounding traction. Know the ceiling: as the data above shows, even a strong owned domain is a small share of a category's citations. Owned pages are the destination that earned and community mentions send buyers to, not the whole strategy. Do them well; don't over-invest.

Specific actions:

  • Publish specific, sourced facts inside long-form pages. Named prices, dates, integrations, limits, and cited evidence give an engine something concrete to quote. In a 252,000-trial controlled study, explicit price information and a recent timestamp consistently raised a source's chance of being cited first, while formatting-only edits had little impact (What Gets Cited, arXiv 2605.25517).
  • Build methodology pages. When you publish a number, document the sample, the engines, the prompt set, the time window, and the limitations on a standalone page. It gives journalists, analysts, and answer engines something checkable to cite when the number gets repeated.
  • Add visible FAQ blocks to high-intent product pages, written for a human reader. Add FAQPage markup only when it mirrors the visible questions: Google stopped showing FAQ rich results in May 2026, and its AI features need no special schema. (More in Why llms.txt Is Not a Strategy.)
  • Show an honest last-updated date on evergreen pages. The same controlled study found a recent timestamp helped consistently, but the date only counts if the content behind it actually changed.
  • Ship a fair comparison hub: your product against each major competitor, on a consistent template, including the competitor's real strengths. Self-ranking "best of" lists backfire: in Lily Ray's June 2026 analysis of 100 B2B queries, Google AI Overviews cited brands' own self-promotional listicles yet left the brand out of the recommendation 224 of 323 times.

Earned layer

Goal: place your brand inside the editorial domains AI engines already cite. Editorial was 10.3% of citations in our category study, and independent research finds AI search leans toward earned, third-party sources over brand-owned content (Chen et al.).

Cost / time / specialist: an external PR specialist or boutique agency ($3,000–$10,000/month for B2B mid-market), plus founder or executive time for interviews and podcasts (2–4 hours per placement). Timeline: 6–12 weeks before the first earned placement, longer for analyst inclusion. This layer usually needs a specialist; generalist marketers tend to underperform here.

Specific actions:

  • Editorial PR: target 1–2 trade press placements per month. Identify the 5–10 trade publications already cited in your category's AI answers (a citation audit gives you this list). Pitch data, not announcements: trade writers cover original findings, and launches rarely earn independent coverage.
  • Analyst relations. Forrester, Gartner, IDC, and vertical analysts shape how a category is described. Their reports are usually paywalled, so engines mostly see them through press coverage and licensed excerpts, but a quote in an analyst note compounds for years. Budget at least quarterly briefings if your category is covered.
  • Guest essays on partner blogs. A 1,500-word original essay on a partner's domain, with your byline and one contextual link back, reaches a domain with its own citation history. It is often a better use of a month than another post on your own blog.
  • Podcast appearances. Aim for one founder or executive appearance a month on podcasts that publish show notes or transcripts, since the indexable text is what an engine can cite. Pitch a specific data point, not a generic "about our company" angle.
  • Pursue Wikipedia carefully and honestly. Wikipedia was one of the three most-cited domains in our category study and the most-cited domain in 11 of 12 languages in the Zatuchin study. But you cannot create a page for yourself, you cannot pay someone to write a promotional version, and Wikipedia's organizations notability guideline explicitly excludes press releases, sponsored content, and paid placements as evidence of notability. The path: earn significant independent secondary coverage first, then let an unaffiliated editor decide whether to write the page. (See the FAQ below.)

Community layer

Goal: build a legitimate presence in the community surfaces AI engines treat as proxies for user experience, primarily Reddit, G2/Capterra, YouTube, and category forums. Community was 11.2% of citations in our category study, and that share is now engine-dependent: ChatGPT stopped showing Reddit citations in August 2026 while Google AI Overviews held steady, and Claude has never cited Reddit. Plan this layer per engine and expect part of its return as influence rather than attribution. See our citation study for the engine-by-engine follow-up.

Cost / time / specialist: an in-house community manager, or the founder at 4–6 hours a week. About $0–$500/month in tooling (review collection, video editing). Timeline: 8–16 weeks to compound. Specialist note: the founder is usually the right voice in years 1–2; outsourcing community to an agency typically reads as inauthentic to the communities and the people in them. For the longer pattern library, see Reddit, G2, and Community Sources for AEO.

Specific actions:

  • Reddit: 5–10 substantive comments a month on category subreddits, founder-attributed. Substantive means 200+ words that answer an actual question. Disclose your affiliation every time ("I work on this, happy to share what we've seen"); subreddit rules and Reddit's spam enforcement punish undisclosed promotion. The full playbook is in The Reddit AEO Playbook.
  • G2 / Capterra / TrustRadius: keep a current base of verified reviews on each platform your buyers use. Buyers weigh them directly: review sites edged out AI chatbots (38% vs. 37%) as the top shortlist influence in G2's July 2026 report, and 45% of buyers in G2's March 2026 survey called review-site citations the most confidence-inspiring signal in an AI answer. (G2 sells review visibility, so weigh its numbers accordingly.) Aim for 20+ recent verified reviews per platform. Beyond presence and recency, there's no public evidence that review volume or star rating moves AI answers.
  • YouTube: one short video a week if Google's AI surfaces matter in your category. In our category study, YouTube citations came mostly from Google AI Overviews and Gemini and barely at all from ChatGPT or Claude, so fund video for the engines that cite it. Engines read titles, descriptions, and transcripts; write them for retrieval.
  • Category-specific forums. Every B2B vertical has 2–4 forums or Slack communities where buyers actually congregate. Show up under your real name, answer questions, and let link-sharing happen organically (not in your first three messages).
  • LinkedIn long-form posts. LinkedIn articles are public, indexable pages, and in our own monitoring they show up as cited sources on B2B prompts. Treat LinkedIn as a publishing surface for one strategic essay a month, not a daily distribution channel.

Sequencing: what to do first

A common mistake is starting all three layers at once in month one, without a baseline. Earned and community both take 6–12 weeks to compound, which means if you start them late, you spend Q3 wishing you'd started in Q1. The right sequence for most B2B teams:

Month 1: Establish a baseline. Run a citation audit. Know your starting distribution per layer and per engine, your top 10 cited URLs (yours and competitors'), and which of your prompts return zero brand mentions today. Without baseline data you cannot tell what's working in month 6.

Month 2: Build owned-layer quality. Publish 4–6 strong articles with specific, sourced facts (comparison tables, named data, methodology pages, visible FAQs). Refresh your top 10 existing pages with current facts, honest dates, and inline citations. This is the foundation earned and community placements link back to; don't pitch trade press a story that links to a 2024-dated blog post.

Month 3: Open the earned layer. Pitch 1–2 trade press stories built around a proprietary data point. Book one podcast appearance. Brief one analyst. Most of these placements land in months 4–5, which is why month 3 is the right start.

Months 4–6: Activate community. Start a Reddit cadence (founder-attributed, disclosed). Run a review-collection campaign (have customer success ask in QBR follow-ups; never pay for reviews or tie an incentive to a positive rating). Add one YouTube short a week if Google's AI surfaces matter for your category. Build forum and LinkedIn presence.

The reason for this sequence: owned is the foundation that earned and community link back to. Earned and community work harder when the destination page is current, specific, and well-shaped. And both compound on a 6–12 week lag, so earned work should be in flight by the end of month 3 and community by month 4, to see results within two quarters.

What you cannot do

This is a non-negotiable section. The line between aggressive AEO and brand-destroying AEO is bright, and the platforms enforce it.

Four tactics are off the table. Each carries a cost if caught that far exceeds the value of the placement, and Google's May 2026 guide says seeking inauthentic mentions "isn't as helpful as it might seem." We will not recommend any of them, and we'll say so publicly when vendors do.

  • No paid Reddit accounts, no comment-seeding agencies, no fake personas. Reddit bans accounts and removes content for spam and vote manipulation, and a brand caught astroturfing becomes the story in the thread.
  • No bought G2 / Capterra / TrustRadius reviews. The platforms run review-validation checks and remove inauthentic reviews. In the US, the FTC's rule on fake reviews, in effect since October 21, 2024, allows civil penalties for buying fake reviews or for incentives conditioned on positive sentiment.
  • No PR placements pretending to be earned. Sponsored content labeled as sponsored is fine; sponsored content presented as editorial is deception. Wikipedia doesn't accept it as evidence of notability, and Google's spam policies now explicitly cover attempts to manipulate generative AI responses. See Do Sponsored Articles Move AI Answers? for what disclosed paid placements can and can't do.
  • No Wikipedia self-creation, sock-puppet editing, or undisclosed paid editing. Wikipedia's terms of use require paid editors to disclose, and conflict-of-interest violations can get accounts blocked and the article deleted, with a public record of what happened.

Measuring source-layer growth

A source-layer program needs a quarterly scorecard. Run these four numbers each quarter on a stable prompt set (the same prompts each cycle, so deltas are real), and report each one per engine, because August 2026 showed one engine can drop a source while the others keep it. The fourth number is the one that aligns with revenue: a brand can grow its owned-citation count and still lose share if competitors grow faster on the earned and community layers.

  • Owned: number of cited URLs from your domain across the prompt set.
  • Earned: number of cited URLs from editorial domains in any answer that mentions your brand by name.
  • Community: number of cited URLs from Reddit, G2/Capterra, YouTube, or category forums where your brand is mentioned.
  • Total brand reference rate: percentage of all citations across the prompt set that reference your brand in any source: yours, editorial, or community.

Why quarterly, not monthly

Refresh quarterly, not monthly. AI answers are noisy: SparkToro's January 2026 study of 2,961 runs found less than a 1 in 100 chance that two runs return the same list of brands, and in our own repeated runs, the set of sources cited for the same prompt changes from day to day. Short-window deltas will mislead you. Quarterly windows match the compounding rate of earned and community work, and any change is an observation to investigate, not proof that a specific action caused it.

FAQ

How long until source-layer work pays off?

Owned layer: 60–90 days for compounding. Earned layer: 90–180 days from first pitch to measurable citation change (analyst-driven change can take 12 months). Community layer: 90–120 days for Reddit and YouTube on the engines that cite them (Google AI Overviews for Reddit; Google AI Overviews and Gemini for YouTube), not ChatGPT citations since August 2026; review-profile work shows up faster (4–8 weeks) once you have a current base of verified reviews. These are planning windows from our work, not guarantees. Plan for a 6-month horizon before you judge the program.

Is Wikipedia worth pursuing?

Yes, if you can earn it. Wikipedia was one of the three most-cited domains in our AEO-category study and the most-cited domain in 11 of 12 languages in a 167,551-citation academic study. But Wikipedia is not something you can buy. The path is: build genuine notability through independent third-party coverage (multiple trade publications, analyst reports, books, academic papers), then let an unaffiliated editor write the page. Avoid agencies that promise to "create your Wikipedia page"; undisclosed paid editing breaks Wikipedia's terms of use and can get both the page and the accounts removed.

How do I get a TechRadar (or equivalent trade press) mention?

Pitch data, not product news. Trade press writers are short on original data and overrun with launch announcements. A pitch framed as "we analyzed X data points on Y question, and here are three findings that contradict the conventional wisdom" lands. A pitch framed as "we just launched feature Z" doesn't. Your own AEO audit data works if it's proprietary and defensible, with a methodology page behind it. Build a relationship with 2–3 writers in your category before you ever pitch.

Do reviews on G2 / Capterra really matter for AI search?

They matter to buyers, and they're one of the sources engines read; the evidence that review counts or star ratings move AI answers is thin. G2's July 2026 report found review sites edged out AI chatbots (38% vs. 37%) as the top shortlist influence, though G2 has a commercial stake in that finding. Practical translation: get to 20+ recent verified reviews on each platform your buyers use, keep them current, then stop chasing volume and reinvest the energy in earned and community work.

What about LinkedIn newsletters?

LinkedIn articles are public, indexable pages, and in our own monitoring they appear as cited sources on B2B prompts. Treat LinkedIn as a publishing surface: one well-researched 1,500-word post a month per executive author beats five short posts a week. Use a real person's byline; it's more credible to readers and easier to attribute.

Should we hire a PR agency or build earned-layer work in-house?

Boutique PR specialists usually outperform generalist agencies for AEO purposes, because they have relationships with the specific writers your category's AI answers already cite. If your team is under 25 people, an external specialist on a $3,000–$10,000/month retainer is usually cheaper and faster than building the function in-house. Above 50 people, a hybrid (an in-house communications lead plus external help for category outreach) becomes the right pattern.

Where does paid media fit?

Mostly outside the source-layer strategy. A disclosed sponsored article on a domain engines already cite for your category can work as a corroboration asset, but it's discounted like a self-claim, and undisclosed placements break platform rules. Paid media still works for brand and demand; just don't treat it as a shortcut into the source layer. See Do Sponsored Articles Move AI Answers?

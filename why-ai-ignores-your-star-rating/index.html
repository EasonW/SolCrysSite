Your Star Rating Gets You Shown in AI Answers. Your Review Text Gets You Picked.

Star ratings do reach AI answers, but only through the data each engine can use: Yelp data in ChatGPT (licensed since July 23, 2026) and Perplexity, and Google Maps and Business Profile data in Google's AI Mode, Gemini, and Ask Maps. There, a rating is displayed and can influence who makes the list. What it can't do is say why you fit the question; that sentence comes from review text and business attributes. So the fix for a business that wins Google Maps but goes unnamed in ChatGPT is rarely more stars. It is a complete profile on the platform each engine reads, genuine reviews with text collected within each platform's rules, and replies and profile details that state the specifics in your own words.

Direct answer

Star ratings do reach AI answers, but only through the data channels each engine has: ChatGPT through a Yelp licensing deal announced July 23, 2026; Perplexity through a Yelp integration announced in March 2024; and Google's AI Mode, Gemini, and Ask Maps through Google Maps and Business Profile data. In those channels a rating is displayed, and it can influence who makes the list. What it can't do is say why you fit the question. The sentence that explains a pick ("known for same-day repairs," "reviewers mention honest pricing") comes from review text and business attributes.

So a 4.7 built on star-only reviews can get you considered and still leave the model nothing to say. And the rating that travels depends on the engine: your Google average feeds Google's surfaces, while ChatGPT's licensed local data is Yelp's. Strong on Google Maps but unnamed in ChatGPT? Check your Yelp presence and whether your reviews have text. For the wider strategy, see local business AEO.

Why a rating can't explain a pick

A star rating is a number in a structured field. It says you're well liked; it doesn't say for what. An assistant can show "4.7 stars" next to your name, and some use ratings to decide who makes the list at all, but the sentence that tells a buyer why you fit their question, such as "known for its thin crust" or "reviewers mention the fast, honest service," comes from review text or from structured attributes. No text, no reason, and an answer that needs a reason will tend to name the business that has one.

That is why Maps rank and AI recommendations diverge. Google's local pack ranks mainly on relevance, distance, and prominence, using signals it can count. An AI answer is assembled from what the engine can retrieve and read about each business, and beyond the number, what it reads is language:

Review text is the local corroboration layer

There's a deeper reason text matters, and it's the same reason third-party sources carry so much weight in AI answers generally: answers lean on what others say about you more than on what you say about yourself. You can put "best pizza in town" on your own page, but you're the most biased source about yourself. A reviewer writing "best thin crust I've had in [city]" is independent, quotable evidence for that exact question. That's corroboration in local form (see AI cites consensus, not authority).

Review text is the community source layer for a local business, and the one you control least (see owned, earned, and community sources). Star-only reviews give the model volume but no evidence it can cite; one specific sentence from a real customer can do more for an AI recommendation than a hundred silent five-star ratings.

The fix: earn quotable reviews within each platform's rules

You don't need more stars as much as you need genuine review text on the platforms each engine reads, plus your own words in the places you control. The platforms' rules shape how: Google lets businesses ask for reviews but prohibits requesting specific content, soliciting only happy customers, pressuring people on the premises, and offering incentives. Yelp asks businesses not to request reviews at all and filters reviews that look solicited. Five moves that work inside those rules:

  • Ask every customer for an honest Google review in their own words. Every customer, not just happy ones, and without suggesting what to say. Inviting a sentence about their experience is fine; scripting it is not.
  • Don't ask for Yelp reviews; make the Yelp page complete. A "find us on Yelp" sign is fine. The levers are accurate categories, services, hours, photos, and specialties, plus thoughtful replies. Because Yelp is the local data licensed to ChatGPT, a thin Yelp page is now a gap in ChatGPT's local data, not just a directory entry.
  • Reply to reviews, restating the specifics in your own words. Your reply sits on the same page, so a reply like "glad we could get your AC running the same day" puts the detail there even when the reviewer wrote a single line. Keep it genuine and specific to that customer, not keyword-stuffed.
  • Put the specific claim on surfaces you control. State what you want to be known for in your Business Profile services and attributes, your Yelp specialties, and your own site ("same-day AC repair in [city]"). Reviews then corroborate a claim that already exists.
  • Make the specific thing true and memorable. The durable way to get "came the same day" into reviews is to come the same day and make it visible. People describe what stood out; give them something that does.

What not to do

Earn genuine text; don't manufacture it. Don't buy reviews, write them yourself, or trade discounts for them: beyond the platform bans, the FTC's rule on fake reviews, in effect since Oct. 21, 2024, prohibits buying reviews and incentivizing reviews of a particular sentiment. Don't ask only happy customers, and don't hand anyone a phrase to include; both break Google's policy. And don't stop collecting ratings: they still win the map pack, and engines display them. The review's text simply does a job the rating never did.

A worked example (illustrative)

Illustrative scenario only. A metro HVAC company we'll call Northwind Air (not a real company) ranked #2 in the local pack with a 4.8 and more than 400 Google ratings, almost none with text, and a Yelp page with little beyond an address. ChatGPT and Perplexity answers to "best AC repair in [city]" didn't name it.

The competitor that was named had a third of the ratings but dozens of text reviews on Google and Yelp saying things like "came same day" and "honest about the price." Northwind began asking every customer, not just satisfied ones, for an honest Google review in their own words; completed its Yelp page (services, hours, photos) without asking for Yelp reviews; stated same-day service on its Business Profile and website; and replied to text reviews, restating the specifics. Rerunning the same questions two months later, it appeared in some same-day and pricing answers: an observation, not proof of cause, but consistent with finally giving the engines something to quote.

One funnel, not two

Ratings matter more in AI answers than the first version of this page said: engines display them wherever they have the data. The shift is that a second signal sits on the same reviews: their language. Track both: star average and review velocity on Google and on Yelp, and whether the phrases that describe you exist in text engines can read. A 4.7 with specific, genuine review text on the platforms your buyers' engines use can win both games. A 4.7 with no text, or a strong Google profile beside an empty Yelp page, wins one and quietly loses the other.

FAQ

Do star ratings matter for AI search?

Yes, more directly than they used to. ChatGPT can show Yelp ratings under a deal announced July 23, 2026, Perplexity has a Yelp integration, and Google's AI Mode, Gemini, and Ask Maps use Google ratings. But a rating shows how well liked you are, not what you're good at; the reason an answer gives comes from review text and attributes. Build ratings on each platform an engine reads, and make sure the reviews say something.

Does ChatGPT use Yelp or Google reviews?

The local data ChatGPT licenses is Yelp's (reviews, ratings, photos, and business details, shown with Yelp branding) under a non-exclusive deal reported by Axios on July 23, 2026. ChatGPT can still surface other sources through web search, but a strong Google review profile doesn't carry into its licensed local data. Google's AI surfaces use Google's data.

Why does my business rank on Google Maps but not show up in ChatGPT?

Because they draw on different data. Google's local pack ranks businesses mainly on relevance, distance, and prominence, using your Google Business Profile and Google reviews. ChatGPT's licensed local data comes from Yelp, plus whatever its web search retrieves. A strong Google profile next to a thin Yelp page, or reviews with no text, leaves ChatGPT little to work with, so it names a business whose reviews describe the thing the question asked for.

What kind of reviews help a local business get recommended by AI?

Genuine reviews with specific text. A review that says "quickest same-day repair in [city]" is independent, quotable evidence for that exact question. Generic praise or a silent five-star rating gives the model nothing to lift. Invite every customer to write an honest review in their own words where the platform allows it (Google does; Yelp asks businesses not to request reviews), and never script what they say.

Should I reply to my Google reviews for AEO?

Yes, on Google and on Yelp, and restate the specific detail in your own words when you do. Your reply sits on the same page the engine reads, so "glad we could get your AC fixed the same day" puts that phrase there even if the reviewer left one line. It's one of the few parts of your review profile whose wording you fully control; keep it genuine rather than keyword-stuffed.

Are more reviews or better review text more important for AI?

Both, for different jobs. Volume and average support the rating the local pack ranks and AI answers display; text supplies the reasons an answer gives. If you're winning Maps and losing the AI answer, the missing ingredient is usually text, or a profile on the platform that engine reads, not more stars.

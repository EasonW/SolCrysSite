AI agents now try to buy on your site. What to check before Black Friday

AI agents from OpenAI and Google can now pay on store sites, and OpenAI says some stores block them without meaning to. Here is what to check before the sale.

Updated 2026-11-17

Questions this guide answers

  • Does my bot protection block ChatGPT Wallet purchases?
  • How do I allow Google-Agent in my CDN or firewall?
  • How do I test whether an AI agent can buy from my store?
  • How do I stop AI agents from buying on my site?

Direct answer

Most stores that sell to consumers should let verified shopping agents through, then prove it with a test purchase before Black Friday on Nov. 27. An agent's purchase can fail in 3 places: your bot protection or CDN rules, the cart and checkout requests behind the page, and your payment risk checks. Check all 3, and judge them by a real agent run, not by the settings.

If you don't want agents buying, say so in your terms and enforce it in your bot manager. Robots.txt won't do it: Google says its user-triggered fetchers, Google-Agent among them, "generally ignore robots.txt rules."

What changed in November

Both companies shipped agent checkout this month.

  • Nov. 13, ChatGPT Wallet. ChatGPT's agent (OpenAI calls each one a dot) can now pay on a merchant's own site with a payment token after the user approves the purchase. It starts with ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the U.S. OpenAI tells businesses to "configure your CDN or firewall to recognize signed requests using Web Bot Auth." Web Bot Auth signs each request so your CDN or firewall can confirm, using OpenAI's public keys, that it came from ChatGPT. The Commerce Partner Playbook has the steps.
  • Nov. 17, Google. Google published Allow Google-Agent traffic, a guide to CDN, firewall, and bot-detection settings for agents running on Google infrastructure. In a separate post that doesn't name a user agent, Google said Gemini can now check out through its Google Pay integration, which uses the Agent Payments Protocol (AP2), but "on some sites, Gemini can make it most of the way through and then hand the screen back to you to click the final button."

Decide whether you want agent purchases

OpenAI says Wallet users "transact directly with businesses, which keep their customer relationships and control over pricing, fulfillment, returns, and customer service." The order comes through your own checkout.

The exceptions are businesses with a reason to keep every purchase human, such as limited-quantity drops, age-restricted goods, or terms that already bar automated buying.

Check 1: bot protection and CDN rules

Run the general checks in Can AI agents use your website? first. Here the question is narrower: does your bot manager recognize the verified agent, and does your allow rule win? OpenAI says its agent's cloud browser needs no allowlist, but "your security settings may unintentionally block these visits."

Google names what can override an allow rule: blocks on whole cloud or data-center networks (ASNs), managed bot rules, and category-level AI-agent blocks. Put the allow rule ahead of them, make it skip CAPTCHAs and JavaScript challenges as well as blocks, and make sure your load balancers and proxies pass on the client IP, user agent, and signature headers.

If a hosting or commerce platform runs your CDN and bot protection, ask it. Google's advice is to "contact your hosting or security provider to see if they support Web Bot Auth verification."

Check 2: cart and checkout requests

An agent that can see your product pages can still be stopped at the cart. Google says the exception has to cover on-site search, filtering, detail pages, add-to-cart, booking, and the XHR, GraphQL, or api.* endpoints behind them. If your cart, checkout, or API runs on its own hostname or rule set, confirm the allow rule reaches it.

Rate limits are the second trap. Many Google-Agent users share one pool of IP addresses, so Google asks you to exempt or account for its verified ranges in per-IP limits and to keep IP rules in sync with its current list.

Check 3: risk and fraud declines

A purchase can clear every wall and still fail at payment. OpenAI says Wallet's payment tokens "work with existing checkout flows, but risk systems may still flag legitimate purchases made by agents, leading to declines or cancellations." Ask your fraud-prevention and payment providers how they recognize these purchases. OpenAI says it is working with Forter, Riskified, Sardine, Signifyd, Adyen, Checkout.com, and Stripe on this.

Test it; don't assume

A request you send with an agent's user agent tests the name, not the signature or the IP address, so it can't show how your bot protection treats the real agent.

  • Read your logs. In your CDN or bot manager, filter for the agents in the table and note what each request got: allowed, challenged, or blocked.
  • Run a real purchase. Have a colleague with ChatGPT Pro in the U.S. ask ChatGPT's agent to buy a cheap item, and do the same with Gemini where its tasks are available. Note where each run stopped: a challenge page, a dead add-to-cart, a hand-back at the final button, or a decline. Cancel the orders, and rerun after each fix.

If you don't want agent purchases

Put the rule in your terms first. United Airlines did this on Nov. 11, Search Engine Journal reported: its terms now require written permission for any "Automated Agent," software that "searches, books, buys or manages," even when a person approves its actions, while search engines indexing public pages stay exempt. SEJ found that United's robots.txt named no AI agents; the rule lives in the terms.

OpenAI doesn't yet let site owners control which actions agents take, so browse-but-don't-buy has to come from your own page-level access controls. To keep agents out entirely, set the same agent entries in your bot manager to block. That step is our inference: the OpenAI and Google guides cover allowing agents, not blocking them. Keep search crawlers allowed if you still want AI answers to cite you (Search, agent, or training? covers which).

Checklist before Nov. 27

Step 1 is for everyone. Steps 2 to 5 are for stores that allow agent purchases.

  • Decide: allow agent purchases, or bar them in your terms and bot manager. If you bar them, run one agent purchase to confirm it's refused, then stop here.
  • Put the agent allow rule ahead of data-center, managed-bot, and AI-category blocks.
  • Extend it to cart, checkout, and API endpoints, and to per-IP rate limits.
  • Ask your fraud and payment providers how they treat agent purchases.
  • Run a real agent purchase, fix where it stops, and run it again.

FAQ

Do I need a product feed or a ChatGPT plugin for agents to buy from my site?

No. OpenAI says browser access "does not require a plugin or product feed." A feed helps ChatGPT find your products; Businesses in ChatGPT covers claiming your listing.

Does allowing ChatGPT's agent also let in OpenAI's crawlers?

No. OpenAI says these visits happen on a user's behalf, are separate from its crawlers such as OAI-SearchBot, and an allow rule for them doesn't change how your site treats other traffic.

Who handles a wrong agent order?

Like any other order. OpenAI says customers are "ultimately responsible for the actions they take."

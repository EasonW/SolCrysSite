Manual ChatGPT spot-checks — when they're useful and when they break

Opening chat.openai.com and asking "what are the best [category] tools?" works as a gut-check, but it breaks fast as soon as you need anything systematic. The two reasons:

  • Variance per session. ChatGPT's answers vary across sessions, accounts, and even minutes — sometimes due to model updates, sometimes due to the routing layer, sometimes due to plug-in availability. A manual spot-check can't average that variance.
  • No structured capture. A human eye reading 10 ChatGPT answers will miss roughly half the competitor mentions and most of the citation links. The parsing layer in a real tracker is the boring part that makes the numbers honest.
  • Right use: Quick "are we in the conversation at all?" sanity-check, e.g., before a board meeting or a campaign launch. Anything beyond that needs a tracker.

DIY: scripting your own ChatGPT visibility tracker

Engineering-led teams can build a minimum tracker for under 100 lines of code. The OpenAI API is the underlying call; the rest is prompt management, response parsing, and storage. The tradeoffs are real:

  • Costs you save: License (~$300–$1500/month at the entry plans of the paid platforms).
  • Costs you absorb: OpenAI API tokens ($0.001–$0.010 per prompt depending on model), engineering time to build, ongoing prompt-set curation, response-parsing edge cases.
  • What breaks first: Parsing. ChatGPT mentions competitors with inconsistent spelling, embeds links in different markdown formats, and sometimes refuses to recommend any brand. Parsing without false positives takes weeks of iteration.
  • Right use: Engineering teams that want full control of prompts and storage, AND have an internal owner for ongoing maintenance. Wrong use: marketing teams that want to ship something this quarter.

FAQ

What is the best free ChatGPT visibility tracker?

For a structured, one-shot read on your brand's visibility in ChatGPT, the SolCrys free audit is the best free option in 2026 — 10 buyer prompts, mention rate, share of voice vs. competitors, and citation sources, returned in about 5 minutes. For ongoing daily tracking, there is no genuinely free path; the paid platforms (SolCrys Brand, Profound, Peec) start in the $300–$500/month range.

How can I monitor my brand in ChatGPT for free?

Three free paths exist: (1) run a one-shot SolCrys free audit to get a structured report, (2) manually prompt ChatGPT with your category questions for a qualitative spot-check, or (3) sign up for a free trial on a paid platform if you're already in vendor evaluation. None of the three give you ongoing daily tracking — that capability is paid across all major platforms.

Is the SolCrys free audit actually free, or is it a trial?

It's actually free, not a time-bound trial. Re-running on the same domain requires the paid Brand plan, which adds daily tracking, custom prompts, and three more engines (Gemini, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews).

Can I track my brand in Gemini and Perplexity for free too?

No major platform offers a free tier covering Gemini and Perplexity in 2026. The SolCrys free audit covers ChatGPT only; the same applies to Otterly's free dashboard tier. Multi-engine tracking is the structural reason the paid plans exist — running a daily scan across four engines on a useful prompt set has real API and infrastructure cost.

Are manual ChatGPT spot-checks a substitute for a tracker?

For a one-off qualitative gut-check, yes. For systematic measurement, no. Manual checks can't capture cross-session variance, miss roughly half the competitor mentions on a careful reading, and don't aggregate over time. The structured part of a real tracker — fixed prompt set, consistent timing, mention parsing — is what makes the numbers comparable.

Can I just script this against the OpenAI API myself?

Yes, if you have engineering capacity and an internal owner for ongoing maintenance. The hard part isn't calling the API; it's curating a buyer-intent prompt set, parsing inconsistent brand mentions and link formats out of model responses, and managing storage. Most marketing teams that try the DIY path end up either abandoning it or buying a paid tracker within 90 days.

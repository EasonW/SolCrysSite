AEO for Mid-Market Companies (2026): Buyer's Guide for 100-1,000 Employee Teams

A mid-market AEO platform (for 100–1,000 employee companies) should cost roughly $400–5,000/month, cover at least four of the major answer engines (ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews / AI Mode, Gemini, and Perplexity or Claude), support a starter set of 50–100 tracked prompts, expose URL-level citation data, let your data out through export or API, and include an action layer your in-house team will actually use.

Direct answer

A mid-market AEO platform (for 100–1,000 employee companies) should cost roughly $400–5,000/month, cover at least four of the major answer engines (ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews / AI Mode, Gemini, and Perplexity or Claude), support a starter set of 50–100 tracked prompts, expose URL-level citation data, let your data out through export or API, and include an action layer your in-house team will actually use. The right choice for a mid-market buyer is rarely the enterprise leader and never the free starter. It's the tier that fits a 5–25 person marketing function with a 90-day window to show results.

Why mid-market is the underserved AEO segment

Most AEO vendors built their first product for one of two extremes: a self-serve starter a solo founder buys with a credit card, or an enterprise platform priced for a Fortune 500 procurement cycle. The middle (companies with 100 to 1,000 employees, $10M to $250M in revenue, 5 to 25 marketers, and a head of marketing who reports to a CEO or CMO) got squeezed.

Mid-market is also where the budget actually exists. Gartner's 2026 CMO Spend Survey puts average marketing budgets at 7.8% of company revenue, up from 7.7% in 2025. Its 401 respondents are marketing leaders in North America, the UK, and Europe, and most work at companies with more than $1 billion in revenue, so treat the ratio as a reference point, not a mid-market norm. As a rough illustration, a $50M-revenue business spending at that ratio would have about $3.9M a year for marketing, and a $1,000–3,000/month AEO line item would be under 1% of it, assuming the buyer can find a vendor priced for them.

This guide is written for that buyer: a head of marketing or mid-market CMO who knows AI search matters, has the budget to act, doesn't have a 6-month procurement runway, and doesn't want to be sold an enterprise SKU "to grow into."

The biggest mid-market trap: buying the wrong shape

An enterprise SKU at $15,000/month with a six-week onboarding burns budget on capacity you won't use. A starter SKU at $99/month with 20–25 tracked prompts caps out in your third month, and then you're re-procuring and re-onboarding inside your first 90 days. The mid-market sweet spot is a mid or Pro tier between $399 and $3,500/month that scales prompt volume and engine coverage without forcing an enterprise contract.

What mid-market teams actually need: 6 criteria

These are the criteria we see decide mid-market evaluations. Each is a hard requirement, not a nice-to-have.

1. Data ownership and export

You should be able to export your citation history (URL-level), your prompt set, and your scoring data, by CSV or API, at any time. If the vendor's pitch is "all your data lives in our dashboard forever," walk away: that's enterprise lock-in priced as mid-market. Ask each vendor to show the export during the trial, not describe it. The vendor section below lists what each one publishes.

2. A reasonable starter prompt set: 50–100 prompts

Below 50 prompts, you rarely have enough signal to detect a change in a 90-day pilot. AI answers vary from run to run (SparkToro's January 2026 study of 2,961 runs found less than a 1-in-1,000 chance of the same brand list in the same order twice), so you need enough prompts, and repeated runs of each, to separate a real change from noise. Above 150, you're paying for prompts no one on a 5–25 person team will review. The sweet spot is 50–100 prompts, structured by persona × journey stage × competitor. Vendors that make you "bring your own prompts" without a starter library shift the discovery burden onto your team, which is usually why dashboards go unused after month two.

3. Multi-engine coverage: at least four major engines

The honest engine breakdown matters. ChatGPT Search depends on Bing's index, OpenAI's OAI-SearchBot crawler, and on-demand page fetches. Perplexity is real-time web retrieval. Google AI Overviews and AI Mode are built on Google's Search index and ranking systems, so AEO there is an operating layer on top of SEO (Google's guide says SEO best practices still apply). Gemini is grounded in Google Search. Claude's web search reportedly uses Brave. A mid-market B2B team should cover at least four of these five. Vendors advertising dozens of "engines" are often counting model variants or API endpoints your buyers never use. Score depth per engine, not count.

4. An action layer, not just a dashboard

This is where mid-market buyers most often regret a purchase. A dashboard tells you where you stand. A 5-person content team will read it twice, then never log in again, unless the platform also produces actionable briefs, page-update recommendations, and a way to check whether the fix moved the answer. Mid-market teams don't have a dedicated AEO analyst to translate dashboard data into work. The platform has to do that translation, or it becomes shelfware. See AI Visibility Dashboard vs AEO Execution Engine for the distinction.

5. Integration with existing tools

HubSpot, Salesforce, Slack, and a BI layer (Looker, Tableau, or Mode) are the mid-market baseline. If you can't get AEO data into your weekly marketing report alongside organic and paid, and can't alert the right person when a competitor displaces you on a top prompt, the platform won't live in your team's workflow. It'll live in a tab no one opens. Ask which integrations are native and which mean an export plus someone's time.

6. Clear ROI proof in 60–90 days

Mid-market budgets renew on results, not narrative. The platform should let you set a baseline in week 1, ship at least 3–5 actions by week 6, and re-measure by week 10 with a clean before/after on the tracked prompt set. If the vendor's case studies all show "12-month enterprise transformation," that's a mismatch. Ask explicitly: "Show me a 90-day mid-market example with prompt-level before/after data." And read the answer as an observation, not proof of cause: a July 2026 survey of 45 GEO studies found no technique with a stable, longitudinal, cross-platform causal effect.

The 60–90-day mid-market pilot framework

Mid-market AEO pilots fail when they have no defined endpoint. Here is the framework we recommend to mid-market buyers. It is vendor-agnostic and designed to produce a scale-or-kill decision by day 90.

Days 1–15: Baseline and prompt-set tuning

Set the success criteria before anything ships. Skipping that step is the most common mid-market mistake: without it, the day-90 review becomes a vibes conversation instead of a renewal decision. Our AEO Platform Pilot Playbook has a criteria template.

  • Week 1: load the starter 50–100 prompts and check that they match your real buyer journey (persona × stage × competitor). Add 10–20 prompts in your own category language.
  • Week 2: capture the baseline: mention rate, citation rate, share of voice against your top 3 competitors, and the URLs the engines cite for your category prompts, per engine.
  • Write the success criteria down: "We will ship at least 5 actions by day 45, and we expect mention rate to rise by X percentage points on the top 25 prompts by day 90."

Days 16–45: First content and source actions shipped

  • Pick 5–10 prompts where you're underperforming and the gap is closable in 30 days. Skip prompts where the gap is structural (for example, a B2B SaaS brand chasing a consumer-category prompt).
  • Ship the actions: page updates, new FAQ entries grounded in real data, and source-layer work (review profiles, disclosed answers from real employees in the communities your buyers use, and third-party coverage).
  • Skip the tactics Google's May 2026 guide says its AI features don't need: llms.txt, AI-only schema, FAQ markup without visible FAQs, and chunking. One page per query variation can also violate its scaled-content-abuse policy. None of these is a credible pilot action.

Days 46–75: Re-measure and calculate recovery

  • Re-run the same prompts on the same engines, several runs per prompt, and compare rates to the baseline.
  • Compare before and after for each shipped action: did this specific fix move this specific prompt? For a per-fix formula, see measuring action-to-result recovery. Treat the change as an observation, not proof of cause; engines change their retrieval without notice.
  • Identify which kinds of action worked (for example, source-layer outreach moved 7 of 10 prompts; FAQ refreshes moved 2 of 5) and use that to budget the next 90 days.

Days 76–90: Scale or kill decision

  • If the pilot hit the success criteria you set on day 1, scale: expand the prompt set to 150–300, add a workspace per product line, and lock in annual pricing.
  • If it missed, run a frank diagnostic: is the platform wrong, the team capacity wrong, or the prompts wrong? Teams that kill on insufficient capacity often re-buy 6 months later and succeed; teams that kill on platform fit are usually right the first time.
  • Document the decision in a one-page memo for your CFO. See AEO ROI: Building a CFO-Ready Business Case for the template.

Common mid-market AEO mistakes

These are the patterns we see hurt mid-market buyers most often.

1. Buying an enterprise tier "to grow into." You will not grow into 1,000 prompts and 12 engines in your first year. Pay for what you'll use in months 1–9 and renegotiate at renewal. The unused-capacity tax on an enterprise SKU often equals the entire budget you should have spent on content production.

2. Buying a starter tier and outgrowing it in 3 months. The opposite trap. A $99/month plan with 20–25 prompts looks cheap until a 5-person team realizes it's flying blind on most of the buyer journey. You re-procure, re-onboard, and lose your first quarter. The mid-market band (about $400–5,000/month) exists for a reason.

3. Treating AEO as a side project instead of giving it an owner. The best predictor of mid-market AEO success we see is naming one person, usually a senior content marketer or SEO lead, as the AEO owner. Not full-time; 25–40% of their week. Without that owner, the platform becomes everyone's responsibility, which means no one's.

4. Not setting clear 90-day success criteria. Covered above, but worth repeating. A pilot without numerical success criteria becomes a subscription that renews on inertia.

5. Evaluating vendors only by demo, not by data. Every vendor demo looks great. Ask for the same 10 prompts to be run on your brand across three vendors during the trial, and compare the outputs side by side. The variance will surprise you and sharpen your selection. (See Why Two AEO Platforms Can Disagree: different vendors produce different visibility numbers for the same brand, for documented methodological reasons.)

6. Underweighting source-layer work. Mid-market teams default to "let's update our own pages." Most citations point elsewhere: an academic study of 167,551 citations in AI answers about 128 brands found 85.7% went to third-party domains (Zatuchin, arXiv 2606.25787). Which third parties get cited varies by engine and can change in a week: ChatGPT all but stopped citing Reddit in August 2026 (Search Engine Journal) while Google AI Overviews kept citing it. A pilot that only updates owned content will usually underperform one that splits effort between owned pages and the review, editorial, and community sources each engine actually cites.

FAQ

What's the right team size to start an AEO program at a mid-market company?

Five marketers minimum, with at least one person who can write or commission long-form content. Below five, you don't have the production capacity to act on the diagnostics, and the platform reverts to a vanity dashboard. Above 25, you're approaching enterprise: different procurement, different SKU.

Do I need a dedicated AEO hire?

No, not in year one. The right pattern is a 25–40% allocation from an existing senior content or SEO marketer. Make it explicit in their goals, give them budget for one platform plus freelance writing capacity, and review quarterly. A dedicated hire becomes justifiable once you're managing 200+ prompts across 3+ product lines.

How do I get budget approved for AEO at a mid-market company?

Frame it as a defensive line item against organic-traffic erosion, not as a new growth channel. AI Overviews answer many informational queries on the results page, and AEO is how you stay in the answer when the click doesn't come. Pair the request with a 90-day pilot ceiling (for example, "$10K total over 3 months: platform, 1 piece of pillar content, and 2 source-layer outreach campaigns") and a defined success metric. CFOs approve bounded experiments far faster than open-ended subscriptions. See our AEO ROI Business Case template.

Should I pilot with multiple vendors at the same time?

Yes, if your budget allows: run two vendors in parallel for 30 days on the same 25-prompt set. The data variance alone will teach you more about the market than any demo. Budget the team time for it; a parallel pilot costs roughly 1.5x the people-hours of a single pilot.

How long does it take to see ROI from AEO at a mid-market company?

A realistic plan: 60–90 days to a first measurable change on tracked prompts, assuming you ship 5–10 actions in that window; six months to a defensible weekly report showing AEO as a contributing channel; twelve months for source-layer work and owned-content updates to reinforce each other. These are planning windows, not guarantees, and a before/after change is an observation rather than proof of cause.

What engines should a mid-market B2B team prioritize?

ChatGPT first: G2's 2026 survey found it is the most-used AI chatbot among B2B software buyers in every segment. Then Google AI Overviews / AI Mode and Gemini, then Perplexity or Claude depending on your buyers. For B2B services and consulting, Google's AI surfaces often lead; for SaaS evaluation, ChatGPT and Perplexity; for developer-facing products, add Claude. Four engines is a sensible year-one scope for a mid-market team.

Is AEO the same as SEO?

For Google, largely yes: AI Overviews and AI Mode are built on the Google index and ranking systems, and Google's May 2026 guide says SEO best practices still apply to them. ChatGPT Search (Bing's index, OAI-SearchBot, and on-demand fetches) and Perplexity (real-time retrieval) draw on different indexes, and research finds AI search leans toward earned, third-party sources, so third-party coverage and clear, current factual pages carry relatively more weight there. Mid-market teams should run AEO as an extension of the SEO function, not a replacement.

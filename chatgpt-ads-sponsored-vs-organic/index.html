ChatGPT ads don't buy the answer. Here's how to budget and report both.

ChatGPT ads reached a $1 billion annualized run rate in under 200 days, but what they buy is a labeled unit below the answer; OpenAI says ads do not influence the answer itself, and neither Google nor Microsoft documents a way to buy the organic recommendation in their AI answers. So the Q4 question isn't ads or AEO. Test the ads from paid media, keep funding the sources and facts that decide what the engine says about you, and report organic answer share and sponsored placements on separate lines. Claude has committed to staying ad-free, so on that engine the organic answer is the only answer. One thing changes at the end of October: unless advertisers opt out, OpenAI can rewrite ChatGPT ads and choose their landing pages, which makes your own site's facts part of the paid unit too.

Direct answer

No. On ChatGPT, an ad is a unit clearly labeled as an ad that sits below the end of a response, OpenAI says ads "do not influence ChatGPT's answers," and neither Google nor Microsoft documents a way to buy the organic recommendation in their AI answers. Anthropic has said Claude will stay ad-free. So test the ads if your buyers can see them, but don't fund the test by cutting the work that earns the organic answer. Report the two on separate lines: organic answer share on one, sponsored placements on the other.

One thing changes at the end of October: unless you opt out, OpenAI can rewrite your ChatGPT ad and pick the page it links to. That doesn't touch the answer, but it does make any relevant page on your site a possible landing page for an ad you paid for.

Three things the table tells a buyer

First, every program puts a label between the paid unit and the answer. The line is thinnest in Google AI Mode, where a Highlighted Answer can sit on the recommendation list itself, marked as sponsored. That is the format I'd watch most closely.

Second, what places the ad isn't what shapes the answer. When several ads are eligible, OpenAI ranks them on "a combination of factors, including relevance and advertiser bids." Nothing in that sentence reaches the sources and facts that decide what the model says about you.

Third, the ad reporting is thin. Google Ads doesn't segment ads shown inside AI Overviews, and Microsoft doesn't offer Copilot-specific metrics. If you can't isolate the AI placement inside the ad platform, you can't read it off a blended visibility score either.

Ads buy the slot, not the answer

I have worked both sides of search marketing, SEO and SEM, and the lesson that carries over is that paid and organic are bought with different currencies. Paid is bought with bids. Organic is earned with relevance and trust. Answer engines keep that split, and ChatGPT flips the layout. On a Google results page, paid listings could sit above the organic results. In ChatGPT the answer comes first, and the ad, if one qualifies, sits below it. By the time a buyer sees your ad, the engine has already told them which vendors fit, and whether you are one of them.

OpenAI's help center is blunt about what an ad is: "Ads are paid placements, and seeing an ad doesn't mean OpenAI endorses or recommends the advertiser or its products or services." The recommendation is a separate event, and it's the one buyers act on. We've argued that being cited is not being recommended. Being advertised isn't either.

One line in OpenAI's documentation matters more to me than the run rate: "By default, ChatGPT can't see the ads shown to you." A buyer who wants to ask about your ad has to share it with ChatGPT, and OpenAI says advertisers don't influence what ChatGPT says about an ad that's been shared. My read is that the engine then answers the way it answers everything else, from what it knows and retrieves about you. Your organic footprint becomes the fact-check on your ad.

Google's new AI Mode formats point the same way. Google says both carry "an independent AI explainer," in which its Gemini model "evaluates and synthesizes information about a product or service." Google hasn't said which sources feed that explainer. My working assumption is that they are the same public facts that shape your organic answer, which means thin or outdated facts about you can follow you into the ad you paid for.

From the end of October, OpenAI can rewrite your ad and pick its landing page

On Oct. 20, OpenAI told advertisers it will turn on "creative enhancements" by default for new and existing self-serve campaigns. With the setting on, OpenAI may rewrite your headlines and descriptions, translate your ad text, resize your images, and send the click to a different page on your site than the one you chose. Variations "may be prepared in advance or created in real time based on the conversation," and they can run without you reviewing or approving each one.

The start date isn't settled. OpenAI's notices have given 3 dates, Oct. 29, 30, and 31, so treat Oct. 29 as your deadline. The opt-out is in Ads Manager settings. The account default applies to every campaign, a campaign-level setting overrides it, and OpenAI says existing opt-outs carry over. You can change it at any time, but anything that runs before you do runs unreviewed.

This doesn't move the wall between ads and answers. OpenAI repeated on Oct. 5 that "advertising does not influence the answers ChatGPT provides." What it moves is authorship. Without enhancements, a ChatGPT ad says what you wrote and links where you point. With them on, the wording can be generated for the conversation, and the landing page can be "a relevant page on your website" that OpenAI picks. A retired plan, an expired promotion, or an old price that's still live on your site can now reach a buyer through the paid unit as well as the organic answer.

  • Decide on the opt-out before Oct. 29. If every ad needs legal or brand review, opt out at the account level and turn enhancements on campaign by campaign. If you want to test them, use one campaign with a spend cap and a decision date, the same standard as the rest of your ChatGPT test.
  • Audit the pages OpenAI can send buyers to, not just the ones you chose. Check pricing, plans, promotions, availability, and product claims across your site, because a wrong fact now has 2 routes to a buyer. The fixes are the same ones that correct the organic answer; see how to fix a wrong fact in an AI answer and what AI-referred buyers need from the page they land on.
  • Mark the switch date in your paid report. If click-through rate moves after enhancements go on, you'll need to know whether your copy or OpenAI's ran. After the switch, click a live ad and check that the landing URL still carries your campaign parameters.
  • Keep the lines separate. Enhancements change the sponsored line only. They aren't evidence that your organic answer moved, in either direction.

The budget call: add a line, don't raid one

The $1 billion figure will be in a lot of Q4 decks. One note before it drives your plan: a run rate is current monthly revenue multiplied by 12, as Digiday pointed out when it reported the milestone, and it measures OpenAI's momentum, not your return. Here is how I'd structure the decision.

  • Fund ChatGPT ads from paid media, not from AEO or content. Treat them as a new channel test with a spend cap, the conversions you will count, and the date you will decide. That is the standard every other paid channel has to meet.
  • Keep the organic work funded, because it compounds and the ads don't. An ad runs while you pay for it. A corrected product page, a published customer story, or accurate coverage on a source the engine already cites can keep shaping answers after the flight ends, the same logic behind sponsored articles that work.
  • Check whether your buyers can see the ads. OpenAI doesn't show ads on Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise, or Edu accounts. If your buyers research vendors in a company ChatGPT workspace, your ads won't reach them there. The organic answer will.
  • Check whether your category is eligible. Google doesn't show ads inside AI Overviews for sensitive verticals, including finance, healthcare, and politics, and OpenAI keeps ads away from sensitive or regulated conversations such as personal health. In those categories, the organic answer may be the only lever you have.
  • Count the engine you can't buy. Claude has no ads, so organic is the whole game there. See how to optimize for Claude.

When the ads are the right call

I'm not arguing against the ads. They are a reasonable buy when you already appear in the organic answer and want to be the obvious next click, when you have an exclusive offer for buyers who are ready to purchase (the use case Google built Direct Offers around), or when you sell to consumers and small businesses who use ChatGPT's Free and Go plans.

They are a poor buy when the goal is to cover an absence. An ad below an answer that recommends 3 competitors is a rebuttal, not a recommendation.

Illustrative scenario: the ad that scaled the wrong message

Illustrative scenario only. A project-management software brand is named in 2 of 10 ChatGPT answers to "best project-management tool for a 30-person agency" over a week of repeated runs. When it is named, the answer calls its pricing "enterprise-only," a plan the brand retired last year. Its ChatGPT ads, meanwhile, report a healthy click-through rate.

Scaling the ads buys more clicks from buyers who were just told the product is out of their price range. Fixing the pricing statement on the brand's own pricing page, and on the 2 comparison sites the engine cites, fixes what the engine reads when it builds the answer, whether or not a buyer ever sees an ad. Both moves are worth making. Only one of them can change the answer.

How to report it: organic and sponsored on separate lines

Search teams learned two decades ago to report SEO and SEM on separate lines, with separate budgets, owners, and math. Answer engines need the same split now; my co-founder Eason Wang made it one of his 5 predictions for year two. This is the version I'd bring to a monthly marketing review.

Report lineThe question it answersWhat goes in itSource of truthWhat it can't tell you
Organic answer shareWhen buyers ask, does the engine name us, cite us, recommend us, and describe us correctly?Mention rate, citations (your pages and the third-party sources behind the answer), Recommendation Score, and Answer Accuracy, per engineRepeated runs of a frozen set of buyer prompts on each engineWhether anyone clicked, or what an ad did
Sponsored placementsWhat did we pay for, and what did it return?Impressions, clicks, spend, cost per click, and conversions, per ad platformChatGPT Ads, Google Ads, and Microsoft Advertising reporting, plus your analyticsWhether the engine recommends you; on Google and Copilot, which results came from AI placements

Rules that keep the lines honest

  • Never add them together. A sponsored impression and an organic recommendation are different units. A blended "AI visibility" number hides which one moved.
  • Tag the paid clicks, and recheck after enhancements. Give every ChatGPT ad landing URL its own campaign parameters, so paid clicks don't blend into organic ChatGPT referrals in your analytics. If you leave creative enhancements on, OpenAI can send clicks to a page you didn't pick, so confirm the parameters still arrive.
  • Label the Google line for what it is. Google Ads reports ads inside AI Overviews as Top Ads, with no separate segment, so report "Google Search ads, including AI Overview placements," not "AI Overview ads."
  • Read the organic line as rates. Answers vary from run to run. SparkToro's January 2026 study of 2,961 runs concluded that visibility measured across repeated runs is a reasonable metric and that ranking position is not; see how many runs make a number trustworthy.
  • Report engine by engine. Claude has no paid line at all, ChatGPT has both, and Google's paid line can't isolate its AI placements. One cross-engine number averages those differences away.
  • Keep revenue attribution out of both lines. Neither line proves pipeline on its own. For that question, ask buyers directly; closed-won interviews are the fastest defensible method we know.

In the EU, a public record of ChatGPT ads is due

On Aug. 31, 2026, the European Commission designated ChatGPT a Very Large Online Search Engine under the Digital Services Act, calling it "a hybrid service" because it answers prompts "including by searching the web." OpenAI had reported 159.1 million average monthly active recipients of ChatGPT search in the EU for the 6 months ending March 31, 2026, well over the 45 million threshold. The added obligations apply 4 months after notification, which the Commission puts at January 2027.

One of them affects the sponsored line directly. A very large search engine that shows ads must keep a public, searchable repository of them, with each ad's content, the advertiser, who paid, when it ran, the main targeting parameters, and the number of people reached, for as long as the ad runs and 1 year after. OpenAI expanded ChatGPT Ads to 31 European countries in August. Once the repository is live, your competitors' ChatGPT ads in the EU become something you can look up, and so do yours. OpenAI hasn't said how its repository will work, including whether it will show conversation-specific rewrites.

ChatGPT ads will be in a lot of Q4 plans, and many teams should test them. Just don't let the test be paid for by the work that decides what the engine says.

FAQ

Do ChatGPT ads affect ChatGPT's answers?

OpenAI says no: "Ads run on separate systems from our chat model, and advertisers have no ability to shape, rank, or alter ChatGPT's responses." Ads appear below the end of a response and are clearly labeled as ads. Creative enhancements, which let OpenAI rewrite an ad, change the ad, not the answer. No outsider can audit that separation directly. What you can measure is your organic answer on a fixed set of prompts over time, which is why it belongs on its own report line.

Can you pay to be recommended by ChatGPT?

Not through any program OpenAI has documented. You can pay for a sponsored unit below the answer, and OpenAI ranks eligible ads on factors including relevance and advertiser bids. OpenAI also says seeing an ad doesn't mean it endorses or recommends the advertiser. The recommendation inside the answer is earned through the sources the engine retrieves and the facts it finds about you.

Should we buy ChatGPT ads or invest in AEO?

They do different jobs, so they belong in different budgets. Ads buy a labeled placement while you pay; AEO work shapes what the engine says about you, and that work can keep paying off after a campaign ends. Test ads from your paid-media budget with a spend cap and a decision date, and don't fund the test by cutting the work that earns the organic answer.

Which AI assistants show ads?

ChatGPT shows ads on its Free and Go plans and in logged-out use; OpenAI said on Oct. 21, 2026 that they're available in 89 countries. As of our Sept. 26, 2026 check, Google shows ads above or below AI Overviews wherever they run and inside AI Overviews in English in 12 countries, and it is testing ad formats in AI Mode. Microsoft Advertising serves ads in Copilot's responses. Anthropic has said Claude will remain ad-free.

Who sees ads in ChatGPT?

According to OpenAI's help center, ads may appear on the Free and Go plans and in logged-out use. Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise, and Edu accounts don't get ads, and neither do accounts identified as belonging to people under 18 or Temporary Chats. Ads aren't eligible near sensitive or regulated topics such as personal health, mental health, or politics, and Free users can switch to an ad-free option with lower usage limits and reduced feature access.

Can I see how my Google ads perform inside AI Overviews?

Not separately. Google Ads says it "currently doesn't offer segmented reporting when ads show within Search AI Overviews," and it reports those ads as Top Ads. You also can't target AI Overview placements directly or opt out of them. Ads inside AI Overviews are available in English in Australia, Canada, India, Indonesia, Kenya, Malaysia, New Zealand, Nigeria, Pakistan, the Philippines, Singapore, and the U.S.

Are ChatGPT ads the same as sponsored articles?

No. A sponsored article is content on a third-party site that an engine can read and sometimes cite, so it can feed the organic answer when it runs on a domain the engine already trusts. A ChatGPT ad is a unit outside the answer that, by OpenAI's account, ChatGPT can't see by default. They're different instruments with different measurement; see do sponsored articles move AI answers.

Does SolCrys track ChatGPT ads?

No. SolCrys measures the organic line: mention, citation, Recommendation Score, and Answer Accuracy, per engine, on your tracked prompts. Your sponsored line comes from the ad platforms' own reporting, and keeping the two sources separate is part of the point.

Will ChatGPT rewrite my ads?

Yes, unless you opt out. OpenAI is turning on creative enhancements by default for new and existing self-serve campaigns at the end of October 2026; its notices have given start dates of Oct. 29, 30, and 31. With the setting on, OpenAI may rewrite headlines and descriptions, translate ad text, resize images, and send clicks to a different page on your site, and those versions can run without your review. The opt-out is in Ads Manager settings: a campaign-level setting overrides the account default, and existing opt-outs carry over. OpenAI says ads still don't influence ChatGPT's answers.

Will there be a public library of ChatGPT ads?

In the EU, yes. The European Commission designated ChatGPT a Very Large Online Search Engine under the Digital Services Act on Aug. 31, 2026. Very large search engines that show ads must keep a public, searchable repository of them, including each ad's content, advertiser, dates, main targeting parameters, and reach. The obligations apply 4 months after notification, which the Commission puts at January 2027. OpenAI hasn't published how its repository will work.

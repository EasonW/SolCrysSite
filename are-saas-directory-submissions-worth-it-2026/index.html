Are SaaS Directory Submissions Still Worth It in 2026?

It depends on which job you need the listing to do. Directories now do two jobs: buyers still use review sites to check a shortlist, and some AI engines cite directories in some categories. The old reason to submit, organic referral traffic, has largely collapsed under zero-click search. Whether the AI-citation reason holds for you depends on your category and the engine, and it can change in a week, so the only way to know is to test rather than assume.

Published 2026-06-17 · Updated 2026-09-26

Questions this guide answers

  • Are SaaS directory submissions still worth it in 2026?
  • Do AI engines cite G2, Capterra, and Product Hunt?

Direct answer

It depends on which job you need the listing to do, and whether that job exists in your category. The old reason to submit (organic referral traffic) has largely collapsed under zero-click search. Two reasons remain. Buyers still check review sites when they vet a shortlist, and in some categories AI engines cite directory pages when they answer buyer questions. The first is the more dependable of the two for B2B software; the second is real for some horizontal SaaS categories and close to irrelevant for others.

When we run a Citation Gap Audit at SolCrys, we read the actual URLs the models pull from for buyer-intent prompts. In our own category, classic software directories barely registered in our June 2026 measurement. In other categories they can matter a great deal. Same question, different answer, decided by your category and the engine, which is why the method below matters more than any average.

The old reason directories mattered, and the new reason people hope they do

Directories used to be a discovery and SEO play. You submitted to G2, Capterra, GetApp, or Software Advice because those listings ranked for "best [category] software" and sent referral clicks, and because a high-authority backlink helped your own pages rank.

That value proposition has cratered. The clicks those listings used to earn are drying up across Google: SparkToro's analysis of Similarweb panel data found 68% of US Google searches ended without a click in January–April 2026 (the panel excludes the Google app). Treat that as a dated, point-in-time snapshot, but the direction is clear: the click-traffic reason to submit is mostly gone.

The new hope is citation. If ChatGPT or Perplexity recommends tools by reading directory pages, then a strong listing becomes AI-citation infrastructure rather than a traffic source. Sometimes that hope is justified. Often it is not. The mistake is assuming it generalizes, because the source mix that feeds AI answers is category-dependent and engine-dependent, not universal.

What our own category data showed in June 2026

In our own monitoring workspace, software directories were not a meaningful AI-citation source. The measurement covers our category (AEO and AI-search tools) across five engines (ChatGPT, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity, and Claude) over a rolling 14-day window, spanning 22 buyer-intent prompts (best and top AEO tools, "which tools help with X," discovery, feature-research, and legitimacy questions) and roughly 24,000 citations across more than 2,000 unique domains, as of mid-June 2026.

The directory finding was clear. None of the classic SaaS directories (G2, Capterra, Product Hunt, GetApp, TrustRadius, Software Advice, Gartner) appeared anywhere in the top 60 cited domains for our category. What sat at the top in that window, by source type:

  • User-generated content: Reddit led by a wide margin, with YouTube and LinkedIn also ranking. Read that per engine as of September 2026: Reddit still appears in Google AI Overviews citations, but ChatGPT stopped naming it in August 2026.
  • Editorial: Wikipedia was number two and TechRadar number three, with arXiv and trade press behind them.
  • Competitor and vendor pages: Semrush, HubSpot, Ahrefs, Conductor, and many AEO-tool vendor sites.
  • Independent comparison and roundup blogs: practitioner posts ranking "the best [X] tools."

Two caveats about that data

First, directories were not invisible. G2 did get pulled in our category, but almost entirely through one page: its answer-engine-optimization category grid, cited across Google AI Overviews, Gemini, and Claude. Even then its total volume stayed below the top 60 domains. Capterra and Product Hunt appeared only a handful of times, and on individual product pages rather than a curated list. So the accurate statement is that directories did not crack the top of the list, not that AI never cites them. And the one directory page that did get pulled was a curated category list, not a product-review page, which is a useful tell about what the models reach for.

Second, our prompt set is squarely at the decision stage, so "directories are largely absent where buyers decide" is a fair read for our category. It is a read about our category, not a verdict for yours. It is also a June snapshot: ChatGPT's source mix changed sharply in August 2026 (see the next section), which is one more reason to re-run the test rather than rely on anyone's old table, including ours.

Where directories still earn their place

Directories still do two jobs, and only one of them runs through AI citation. The more dependable job is buyer validation. In G2's 2026 Buyer Behavior Report (July 22, 2026; more than 1,000 B2B software buyers and decision-makers), review sites (38%) narrowly edged out AI chatbots (37%) as the top influence on which vendors make a shortlist, the first time review sites led, even though 82% of respondents had sourced software recommendations from an AI chatbot in the prior 24 months. Read it with the source in mind: G2 runs a review site and has owned Capterra, Software Advice, and GetApp since February 2026, so it has a stake in the result. The direction is still intuitive: buyers use AI to build a shortlist and review sites to check it.

The AI-citation job is conditional, and the evidence on it is thinner than the confident charts suggest. Most published breakdowns of which directory AI engines cite come from AEO and SEO tool vendors, use different prompt sets and engines, and disagree on which directory leads. We don't treat any of them as a benchmark for your category. The closest neutral evidence we know of is about discovery rather than directory citations, and it cuts the same way. A January 2026 preprint, "The Discovery Gap", tested 112 startups from Product Hunt's top 500 across 2,240 queries: ChatGPT (gpt-4o-mini) and Perplexity (sonar) recognized the products by name 99.4% and 94.3% of the time, but surfaced them in open-ended discovery queries only 3.32% and 8.29% of the time. GEO-style optimization showed no correlation with discovery; referring domains and Product Hunt ranking predicted Perplexity visibility. It is a single-author preprint that tested API models rather than the consumer apps, so treat it as directional: being known by name is not the same as being surfaced when a buyer asks an open question.

Which third-party sources an engine cites can also change without notice. In August 2026 ChatGPT all but stopped citing Reddit and began showing fewer sources per answer, while Google AI Overviews, Perplexity, and Gemini were largely unaffected (our engine-by-engine follow-up). In our own tracking across client categories, the share ChatGPT stopped giving community threads went mostly to vendors' official sites, documentation, and government and reference pages. For directory strategy the lesson is the one this page keeps making: a source that feeds one engine this month may not feed it next month, so a directory decision has to be re-tested, not set once.

Worth tracking: G2 announced acquiring Capterra, Software Advice, and GetApp from Gartner on Jan. 29, 2026, and the deal closed Feb. 5, 2026 (about $110 million, per Gartner's 10-K). That puts four of the best-known software review brands under one owner. It is worth watching, but it does not change the test you should run.

  • Buyer validation is the dependable job. Buyers still read review sites to check a shortlist, whether or not an AI engine cites them.
  • Being known is not being recommended. Being findable by name is not the same as appearing when a buyer asks an open question.
  • AI citation is conditional. It depends on category and engine, and a single engine's retrieval change can reshuffle it in a week.
  • The big four now share one owner. G2 has owned Capterra, Software Advice, and GetApp since February 2026.

The method: test which sources the model cites for your category

Do not decide from studies or from this article. Decide from your own buyer questions, because the source mix is category- and engine-specific and only your prompts reflect your category. The test is a manual version of the Citation Gap Audit, and you can run it in an afternoon.

A note on what does not work: bought reviews, fake personas, sock-puppet or paid promotional editing, undisclosed paid placements, and mass submissions to low-quality directories. Google's generative AI guidance says seeking inauthentic "mentions" across the web "isn't as helpful as it might seem," and its spam policies (clause added May 15, 2026) now cover attempts to manipulate generative AI responses in Google Search. Earn legitimate listings and legitimate reviews. For how to engage authentically on Reddit and review sites once you know they matter for you, see our Reddit, G2, and forums playbook.

  • Write your real buyer questions. Use the literal phrasing a buyer types: "best [your category] software," "top [category] tools for [use case]," "[competitor] alternatives," "is [your product] any good." Aim for 10 to 20 prompts at the decision stage, not brand-name lookups.
  • Ask each prompt in each engine that matters to you. Run them through ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews at minimum; add Gemini and Claude if your buyers use them. Engines disagree, so test each one you care about separately.
  • Log every cited URL by source type. For each answer, record the linked domains and tag each as directory, UGC (Reddit, YouTube), editorial or trade press, competitor or vendor page, or independent comparison blog. A simple spreadsheet is enough.
  • Count where the citations actually concentrate. If directories show up repeatedly across your prompts and engines, that is your signal to invest in listings. If Reddit threads, editorial roundups, and comparison pages dominate, those are your real citation surfaces, and a perfect G2 profile will not move the needle much.
  • Submit only where the model already pulls from, or where your buyers already look. Prioritize the directories you saw cited for your category and the review sites your buyers mention in sales calls, get a complete and accurate profile there, then put the rest of your effort into the source types that won the count.

What to do this week

A directory profile is rarely the highest-leverage move, but the test that tells you whether it is takes one afternoon. This week:

  • Run the citation test above on 10 to 20 of your real buyer-intent prompts across at least ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews. Tag every cited URL by source type.
  • Read the count, not the assumption. Decide directory investment from what the engines cited for your category, not from what a martech blog says works on average.
  • Claim and complete your profiles on the directories you actually saw cited or that buyers use, and verify the basics are accurate (category, description, integrations, pricing). Treat this as legitimacy table stakes, not a ranking hack.
  • Redirect the surplus effort to whichever source types won the count (often editorial roundups, independent comparison pages, and, on Google's surfaces, community threads), and use the source-layer strategy guide to sequence it.
  • Re-run the test in 30 days. AI citations move fast: in our own repeated runs, the set of sources cited for the same prompt changes from day to day, so a single run is a snapshot, not a trend.

FAQ

Are software directory submissions a waste of time in 2026?

Not always, but they are no longer a default. The traffic reason to submit has largely collapsed. Buyer validation still holds: in G2's July 2026 survey, review sites were the top shortlist influence, narrowly ahead of AI chatbots. The AI-citation reason only holds where engines actually cite directories for your category. Test your own buyer questions before investing.

Do ChatGPT and Perplexity cite G2 and Capterra?

Sometimes, depending on category and engine, and it changes. In our AEO-category data from mid-June 2026, G2 was cited mostly through one category-grid page and Capterra only a handful of times, well outside the top cited domains. Published cross-category breakdowns disagree on which directory leads, and ChatGPT's August 2026 retrieval change reshuffled which third-party sources it cites. Test your own buyer prompts on each engine you care about.

Why didn't directories show up in your own category data?

For AEO and AI-search-tool prompts across five engines over a 14-day window in June 2026, the top cited sources were Reddit, Wikipedia, TechRadar, vendor and comparison pages, and editorial roundups. (Reddit's position there was largely ChatGPT's doing; ChatGPT stopped naming it in August 2026, as covered in our citation study.) G2 did get cited, almost entirely through its category-grid page, but its volume stayed below the top tier; Capterra and Product Hunt appeared only a handful of times. That is a category finding, not a universal one.

How do I find out which directories matter for my product?

Ask 10 to 20 of your real buyer-intent prompts in ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews, log every cited URL, tag each by source type, and see where citations concentrate. Then add the review sites your buyers mention in sales conversations. Submit to the directories you actually saw cited or heard named, and skip the rest.

Do more reviews help me rank higher in AI answers?

There is no neutral evidence that review volume moves AI rank, and rank in AI answers isn't stable enough to target: SparkToro's January 2026 study of 2,961 runs found less than a 1-in-1,000 chance that two runs return the same brands in the same order. What does hold is that reviews matter to buyers directly: in G2's July 2026 survey, review sites were the top shortlist influence at 38%, just ahead of AI chatbots at 37%. Treat reviews as legitimacy and buyer validation, not a ranking lever.

Is Product Hunt worth it for AI citations?

Product Hunt is mainly a launch-moment tactic with a founder and maker audience rather than buyers. A January 2026 preprint testing 112 Product Hunt startups found ChatGPT and Perplexity recognized them by name 94–99% of the time but surfaced them in open-ended discovery queries only 3–8% of the time; referring domains and Product Hunt ranking, not GEO tactics, predicted Perplexity visibility. In our own category data it surfaced only a handful of times. Use it for a launch, not as a directory-strategy pillar.

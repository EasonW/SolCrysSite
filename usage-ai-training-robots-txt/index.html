The new AI training opt-out in robots.txt: most brands should change nothing

On Oct. 30, 2026, Microsoft, Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic backed a robots.txt rule, __usage_ai-training, for refusing AI training.

Published 2026-10-30 · Updated 2026-10-31

Direct answer

If you want AI answers to cite your brand and you don't mind AI training, change nothing: a site that doesn't add the rule keeps working as before.

Read on only in 2 cases. If you want to refuse training, decide Google-Extended first, or the opt-out can also cost you Gemini app citations. If your pages carry Bing's NOARCHIVE or NOCACHE tags, Bing stops reading them as AI controls in 2027, so check them now.

What was announced, and when each company starts

The rule is 2 lines in robots.txt: User-Agent: __usage_ai-training, then Disallow: /. It doesn't block crawling.

If you opt out, decide Google-Extended first

Google-Extended is Google's robots.txt switch for Gemini training, and it also controls grounding in the Gemini app, where Gemini reads your pages to answer a question and can link to them. Google plans to read the opt-out as a Google-Extended disallow whenever your file doesn't name Google-Extended, so the 2 lines alone can cost you Gemini app citations. Appearing in Google Search, AI Overviews, and AI Mode isn't affected.

To keep Gemini app citations, add 2 more lines, User-Agent: Google-Extended then Allow: /. Gemini training then stays allowed too, because Google has no setting that separates the two. To refuse Google as well, use the 2 lines alone.

Either way, check 4 things in the rest of the file.

  • Company lines. A line for GPTBot, ClaudeBot, or Google-Extended can override the shared rule at that company, so make each one say what you want there.
  • Other AI companies. Apple and others haven't announced support, so their own lines, such as Applebot-Extended, are still the only way to refuse them.
  • Search crawlers. Keep Googlebot, Bingbot, OAI-SearchBot, and Claude-SearchBot allowed, because a crawl block removes you from answers; see which bot does what.
  • The live file. Fetch your site's /robots.txt after the change, in case a CDN such as Cloudflare, or a plugin, writes its own.

If your pages carry NOARCHIVE or NOCACHE

Bing reads these tags as AI controls today: NOARCHIVE keeps a page out of Copilot answers and training, and NOCACHE limits both to the URL, title, and snippet. From Feb. 1, 2027, Bing ignores them on any site where it finds one of its new training or grounding preferences, including the robots.txt rule above, and everywhere after March 1, 2027. Pages that relied on them can then be used in Bing's AI answers and for training unless you replace them.

  • Check whether you have them. Search your page source and response headers for noarchive and nocache. If they're there by accident, from an old template, plugin, or header, remove them now.
  • If you use them on purpose, leave them in place and follow Microsoft's transition steps for the whole site before March 1, 2027. One new preference ends the old tags site-wide from Feb. 1, so a partial switch can expose the rest of the site, and Bing has no temporary setting for training.

FAQ

Do my existing robots.txt rules already opt me out of AI training?

No, except at a company whose own crawler or token your file names, such as GPTBot or Google-Extended. Under the shared rule, User-Agent: * never counts as a training preference.

Should I set Bing's new AI Grounding preference?

No, if you want to be cited. It defaults to Allow, and Microsoft says restricting it means "reduced brand visibility in AI experiences."

Your Google AI numbers moved in November? Separate Google's three changes first

Between Nov. 9 and 12, 2026, Google started a core update, put a new Gemini model into Search, and hit a logging bug in Search Console. A Search Console trend that crosses those dates mixes all three; answers you captured yourself still mix the update and the new model.

Updated 2026-11-17

Questions this guide answers

  • Why did my AI Overviews impressions drop in November 2026?
  • What was the Search Console Generative AI report bug on Nov. 9–11, 2026?
  • Does Gemini 4 Hydrogen change which sites Google's AI answers cite?
  • When should I remeasure after the November 2026 core update?

Direct answer

Don't credit or blame your own content for AI Overview, AI Mode, or Search Console movement between Nov. 9 and about Nov. 24, 2026. Mark that window on every chart. Then compare your last clean week before Nov. 9 with a new baseline taken after the core update finishes, and report that difference instead of the week-by-week line.

To explain what moved, run four checks: drop the bug dates, compare rankings with AI impressions, check whether answers now cite other pages, and compare the same prompts before and after.

What changed, and when

Three changes overlap, and the core update is still rolling out.

  • Nov. 9–11: a Search Console bug. Google says "a logging error caused a decrease in impressions on the Generative AI performance report" for those dates, and that it "affects data logging only." The error shrank the report, so those three days can't tell you whether your pages were also shown less; the core update began on Nov. 10.
  • From Nov. 10: the November 2026 core update. It "applies globally and to all languages." Google's AI features are "rooted in our core Search ranking and quality systems," so a core update can move AI Overview and AI Mode citations as well as rankings.
  • Nov. 12: Gemini 4 Hydrogen in Search. Google put it "in Google Search for all users," with upgraded query fan-out (the related searches the model runs on a question's subtopics) "connecting you to a broader range of helpful links than before." Google's Robby Stein said it handles "your hardest questions in AI Overviews," Search Engine Journal reported.

Check 1: take out the bug dates

Treat Nov. 9–11 as missing data in the Generative AI performance report, not as a drop. Then add your own annotation to the Performance report chart (right-click the date) so anyone reading it later sees the window.

Check 2: rankings vs. AI impressions

Average position in Search Console won't separate the two: an AI Overview "occupies a single position in search results," every link in it gets that position, and those positions are averaged in with the classic results. So check each key page's classic rank on its main queries directly, with signed-out searches or your organic rank tracking, and compare it with the page's impressions in the Generative AI performance report, in your new baseline week against your last clean week before Nov. 9. The free AI reports guide explains what each report counts.

  • Classic rank and AI impressions fell together: the core update is the likelier cause. Treat it as a ranking question first.
  • Classic rank held, but AI impressions moved: the AI answers changed around you. Go to Checks 3 and 4.

Check 3: is fan-out citing other pages?

Broader fan-out lets an answer cite pages that cover one part of the question, such as pricing, setup, or a comparison. On prompts where your citations changed, list the pages cited before and after. If the new ones answer sub-questions your page skips, answer those sub-questions on pages you already have. Don't publish a page per variation: Google's guide says publishing pages like that to manipulate generative AI responses violates its scaled content abuse policy.

Check 4: the same prompts, before and after

Search Console doesn't show which answers cite you. Rerun the prompt set you tracked before Nov. 9, unchanged, in the same location, language, and login state, several times per prompt, and compare how often each of your URLs is cited. If prompts you didn't touch moved as much as the ones you worked on, that points to the engine, unless one source or site-wide change touches both groups.

Run AI Mode checks signed out or on a free account. Ultra subscribers have had Gemini 4 Carbon in AI Mode since Nov. 10, Pro subscribers reportedly by Nov. 16, and AI Mode no longer shows which model is selected. If you track prompts in SolCrys, your AI Overviews captures from before Nov. 9 are that baseline.

When to remeasure

Google marks a core update complete on its Search Status Dashboard; up to 2 weeks from Nov. 10 means around Nov. 24. Take the new baseline from the first full week after that. In the U.S., that week falls next to Thanksgiving (Nov. 26), Black Friday, and Cyber Monday (Nov. 30), so compare traffic and impressions with the same week last year; citation rates on fixed prompts don't depend on demand.

If numbers keep drifting after the new baseline, see why your AI visibility score moves.

FAQ

Will Google restore the missing Nov. 9–11 impressions?

Google's notice doesn't say so. Plan as if they won't come back.

Do these changes affect Gemini, ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Claude?

Hydrogen also arrived in the Gemini app's model menu on Nov. 12, so Gemini answers can shift too. None of the three changes is in ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Claude; if those moved, start with the tracking-break checks.

What changed since our May 2026 edition

"Monitoring-only" no longer describes most vendors: nearly everyone recommends actions, several draft content, and MCP servers are common. What still separates tools is what happens after the recommendation: who drafts, who approves, who publishes, and whether the same prompts are re-run.

  • Ownership: Adobe completed its Semrush acquisition on April 28, 2026. Sitecore announced it had acquired Scrunch on June 3, 2026.
  • Pricing: Profound now lists only a free Trial and custom Enterprise for brands; Peec AI lists Starter at $95, Pro at $245, and Advanced at $495 a month in the US; HubSpot AEO launched at $50 a month; Ahrefs sells Brand Radar standalone from $199 a month.
  • Execution: Conductor launched AgentStack in April 2026, AirOps launched its Quill agent on May 13, 2026, Peec AI added Actions (in beta), Scrunch repositioned around its Agent Experience Platform (AXP) in July 2026, and Profound raised a $180 million Series D at a $1.8 billion valuation on Sept. 15, 2026, to build what it calls an AI Marketer.
  • Data access: Profound, Peec AI, Otterly AI, AirOps, AthenaHQ, Ahrefs, Semrush, Conductor, and SolCrys all document MCP servers. The useful questions are what each exposes, on which plan, and whether it can write.
  • Analyst coverage: Gartner published its first Market Guide for Answer Engine Visibility Tools on March 9, 2026.

What is an AEO tool, and what should it actually do?

An Answer Engine Optimization tool measures and improves how a brand shows up in AI-generated answers across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, Gemini, Claude, and, for retail, assistants such as Amazon Alexa for Shopping and Walmart Sparky. The minimum bar is measurement: which prompts your brand appears in, how often it is cited, which competitors take your share, and which sources the engines pull from.

The higher bar is execution: moving from "we found a gap" to "we shipped an approved fix and re-tested the same prompts." That takes content work that respects approved claims, governance over who publishes what, and a re-test of the same prompt set. Most vendors now recommend and several draft; far fewer combine human approval with a re-test.

The four evaluation axes we use

We score every tool below against the same four axes, the framework from our AI Visibility Platform Buyer's Guide.

  • Measurement coverage: which engines, how often, retail vs generic, and prompt-set quality.
  • Diagnosis: does the tool tell you why a gap exists and which sources are missing, or only that it exists?
  • Execution: does the tool help produce the fix (content brief, page update, FAQ entry, citation outreach), and can it route that work through approval?
  • Verification: does the tool re-run the same prompts after the fix and show whether the answer moved?

How we chose these 12

We chose vendors with a live, documented AEO product as of September 2026 that we see named most often in category roundups and in the evaluations prospects describe to us, and that together cover four distinct jobs. That is not the same as "the 12 best," and it is not a market-share ranking.

We don't rank 1 to 12; the right order depends on the job. A monitoring-only SMB and an enterprise content team will pick different tools and both be right.

Execution-oriented platforms

These tools go beyond measurement: they turn gaps into recommended work and help produce the fix. They differ on governance (who approves), publishing (whether the tool writes to your CMS), and verification (whether it re-runs the same prompts). They demand more operating discipline than a dashboard.

2. Profound

Profound raised a $180 million Series D at a $1.8 billion valuation on Sept. 15, 2026. Its strength is analyst-grade measurement at enterprise scale, and it has moved firmly into execution. Profound Agents are reusable workflows that, per Profound, can connect to your CMS and publish approved content; the company announced an "AI Marketer" teammate alongside the Series D; and its MCP server can both read data and start Agents.

3. AirOps

AirOps started as a content workflow platform and rebuilt around AI search. Its Quill agent (launched May 13, 2026) finds opportunities and turns them into content work through Playbooks with human review and approval. AirOps Insights tracks AI visibility, and the Pro plan connects to CMSs including WordPress, Contentful, Sanity, Strapi, and Webflow.

  • Best for: content-led teams that want production workflows and AI visibility in one tool.
  • Strongest at: content operations at volume, Brand Kits and knowledge bases, CMS integrations, and an MCP server on Pro and Enterprise.

Measurement-first dashboards

These tools lead with measurement: where you appear, who wins instead, and which sources the engines cite. All three now add recommendations, but the fix still happens in your own workflow. They are strong starting points for sizing the problem.

Otterly has the lowest paid entry price in this list ($29 a month) and is quick to start. Every plan tracks ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity, and Microsoft Copilot; Google AI Mode, Gemini, and Claude are paid add-ons. Plans include GEO audits and recommendations, and Standard and above include API and MCP access.

  • Best for: SMB and mid-market teams, and SEO practitioners, who need fast, low-cost prompt tracking.
  • Strongest at: price-to-coverage at the low end, clean per-prompt tracking, and an agency partner program with pitch workspaces and branded Looker Studio reports.
  • Honest limitation: add-on engines raise the real cost (Claude adds $109 a month on Standard), and the work after the report happens outside Otterly.
  • Pricing posture: Lite $29/mo (15 prompts), Standard $189/mo (100), Premium $489/mo (400), Enterprise custom.

5. Peec AI

Peec AI is an analytics-first platform with one of the cleanest dashboards in the category and a fast-growing surface around it: Actions (in beta) ranks site-audit, owned, and earned opportunities with evidence and a suggested brief; a fact-checking feature checks AI answers against facts you set; and its MCP server exposes both read and write tools. Self-serve plans let you choose 3 models, with Perplexity and others available as add-ons, and Enterprise covers up to 13.

  • Best for: marketing and SEO teams that want strong analytics and exports and will act on recommendations in their own workflow.
  • Strongest at: dashboard quality, unlimited users on every plan, Looker Studio and CSV exports, and a broad MCP tool set.
  • Honest limitation: self-serve plans track 3 models before add-ons, and Actions recommends and tracks work but, per Peec, does not write the content.
  • Pricing posture: Starter $95/mo, Pro $245/mo, Advanced $495/mo (US pricing on Sept. 26, 2026; €85, €205, and €425 in Europe); Enterprise custom.

6. AthenaHQ

AthenaHQ prices by credits (1 credit equals 1 AI response) and has the widest model list of any self-serve plan in this guide: 11 models on Starter, including ChatGPT, Perplexity, AI Overviews, AI Mode, Gemini, Claude, Copilot, Grok, DeepSeek, Meta AI, and Mistral. Its Action Center turns opportunities into content, citation-outreach, and commerce workflows.

  • Best for: teams that want broad model coverage and prompt-level benchmarking without an enterprise contract.
  • Strongest at: model breadth, a free Essential tier, a content optimization agent, and an MCP server.
  • Honest limitation: credit pricing makes cost depend on prompts × models × frequency, and API access is a paid add-on on Starter.
  • Pricing posture: Essential free (300 credits), Starter $295/mo (3,600 credits), Enterprise custom.

Sitecore announced its acquisition of Scrunch on June 3, 2026. How to pick the right tool for your team

Start with the job, not the tool. These four steps get most teams to a two-tool shortlist.

  • Step 1: Decide whether you need a dashboard (measure and recommend) or an execution platform (measure, diagnose, route approved work, and re-test). Most teams over-buy dashboards and under-buy execution.
  • Step 2: Decide whether retail or local engines matter. If they drive revenue, treat them as a separate buy on a separate track.
  • Step 3: Decide whether you need integration with a suite you already run (HubSpot, Semrush, Ahrefs, SE Ranking, Conductor, Sitecore) or whether a specialist is worth a second contract.
  • Step 4: Run a 30-day pilot with one or two shortlisted tools on the same prompt set, and grade them on the same four axes: measurement coverage, diagnosis, execution, and verification.

Mistakes we see most often

The patterns we see when teams pick the wrong tool, or use the right one the wrong way.

  • Buying a dashboard when the job is execution. The team gets clean reports and zero shipped fixes.
  • Treating retail and generic AEO as one buy. The mechanics differ, and one tool rarely covers both well.
  • Comparing vendor composite scores to each other. Each vendor calculates its visibility score differently, so the absolute numbers are not comparable.
  • Reading a single run as a result. AI answers vary run to run; judge visibility across repeated runs of the same prompts.
  • Skipping the verification step. If a tool cannot re-test the same prompts after the fix, you cannot tell whether anything moved.

FAQ

Which AEO tool should I buy first?

Run a 30-day pilot of two tools, not one. Pick one execution-oriented platform and one measurement-first dashboard, run both on the same 25 to 50 prompts, and decide based on whether your team actually shipped fixes during the pilot. Teams that ship fixes need an execution platform; teams that only need monitoring should pick the simpler dashboard.

Are SEO-suite AEO add-ons good enough?

For teams already standardized on Semrush, Ahrefs, or SE Ranking, often yes: the incremental cost is low and the integration is real. For mid-market and enterprise teams running a serious AEO program, specialists usually offer more engines, more prompt depth, or an action layer. Re-evaluate every year; the suites are closing the gap.

Do I need both a generic AEO tool and a retail AEO tool?

Yes if marketplace or local revenue is meaningful, no if it is not. Generic AEO platforms cover ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, Gemini, and Claude. Retail tools cover shopping assistants such as Amazon Alexa for Shopping and Walmart Sparky and ChatGPT's shopping results. The engines, data sources, and fixes differ enough that one tool rarely covers both well.

Do AEO tools work for B2B SaaS, or only for ecommerce?

The 12 tools in this guide are built primarily for open-web buying journeys, which includes B2B SaaS and services. They measure the engines B2B buyers use during research: ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, Gemini, and Claude. Retail and local tools (Yotpo Discover, Birdeye Search AI, or SolCrys's Retail AI add-on) are for ecommerce, marketplaces, and multi-location businesses. See our B2B buyer guide for a B2B-specific comparison.

How long does it take to see AI visibility movement after using an AEO tool?

A dashboard gives you a usable baseline within days of configuring a prompt set. Fixes take longer to register because each engine refreshes its retrieval on its own schedule, so plan for a 90-day window before judging a fix-and-verify loop. Measure visibility as a percentage across repeated runs of the same prompts: SparkToro's January 2026 study found less than a 1-in-1,000 chance that two runs return the same brand list in the same order. Treat before/after movement as an observation, not proof of cause.

Can I run an AEO tool alongside my existing SEO platform?

Yes, and most teams do. SEO-suite products (Semrush, Ahrefs Brand Radar, SE Visible) sit next to the SEO seat, and AEO-first platforms run alongside any SEO tool because they measure a separate surface with different signals: mentions, citations, and recommendations. For Google's AI surfaces, strong SEO remains the foundation; Google's May 2026 guide says optimizing for generative AI search is still SEO.

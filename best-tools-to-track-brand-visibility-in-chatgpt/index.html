The measurement problem most "ChatGPT checkers" hide

Plenty of tools marketed as "ChatGPT brand trackers" run a single prompt once. You type your brand, the tool asks ChatGPT, and you see one answer. That is not measurement. That is a screenshot.

Four sources of variance make single-prompt checks misleading:

  • Sampling noise. The same prompt returns different answers from run to run. SparkToro's January 2026 study (2,961 runs of 12 prompts) found less than a 1-in-100 chance that two runs return the same brand list, and less than 1 in 1,000 that they return it in the same order. A one-shot tool can't tell you which side of that distribution you landed on.
  • Model churn. The model behind ChatGPT keeps changing. OpenAI retired GPT-4o and GPT-5 from ChatGPT on Feb. 13, 2026 (OpenAI), made GPT-5.5 Instant the default in May, and moved Free and Go users to GPT-5.6 Luna in August (ChatGPT release notes). ChatGPT also decides per question whether to search the web. The answer your buyer sees depends on which model and path handled it, and no tracker sees that routing.
  • Location. Web results can be localized, so the same prompt asked from two countries can cite different sources. Measure from the markets your buyers are in.
  • Retrieval changes. Engines change what they cite without notice. In the week of Aug. 10, 2026, ChatGPT all but stopped citing Reddit (from 3.83% to 0.52% of citations, per trackers reported by Search Engine Journal), while Perplexity and Google's AI surfaces were largely unaffected. A brand whose ChatGPT visibility rode on Reddit threads saw it move within days without changing anything.

Why a schedule beats a screenshot

This is why a real ChatGPT visibility tool runs a prompt set on a schedule, not a single prompt on demand. SparkToro's conclusion from its study was that visibility percentage across prompts run multiple times is a reasonable metric, and ranking position is not (SparkToro). You want repetition, and a record of which sources were cited on each run.

ChatGPT with search vs. ChatGPT from memory: the distinction most articles get wrong

Before comparing tools, fix this in your head. "ChatGPT" is not one answer path. As of September 2026 there are at least three, and a brand can be visible in one and invisible in another.

SurfaceWhat it actually isLive web retrieval?Cites URLs?

What the three paths mean for tracking

The brand-visibility implication: if a tool only measures "did ChatGPT mention you," ask which ChatGPT. Visibility in memory-based answers reflects what the model learned in training. Visibility in search answers reflects which pages ChatGPT's retrieval finds and trusts, including whether OAI-SearchBot can reach your site; OpenAI says sites that opt out of it won't be shown in ChatGPT search answers (OpenAI). Both matter; they are not the same metric.

Perplexity is simpler: every answer includes citations to the pages it retrieved (Perplexity). Free users get Perplexity's default search; Pro and Max subscribers can choose among models from OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, and others alongside Perplexity's own Sonar (Perplexity). The model behind the synthesis varies by account; the source-layer game is the same.

Reading the table

Cited-source capture is the column that separates real trackers from screenshot tools. Some "ChatGPT mention" products return only the answer text, not the URLs the answer cited. That gap matters because ChatGPT search answers and Perplexity both list sources, and the sources are where your fix work happens. If a tracker can't show you which domains the engines lean on in your category, you can't act on it.

Marketed coverage and actual capture can diverge, for any tool. That includes SolCrys when a prompt set is too small to be representative. Before you rely on a tracker, hand-run 10 of your own prompts and compare. Prompt-set size and run frequency matter more than the vendor logo.

Most trackers now sell something beyond tracking. Profound has agents, Peec AI has recommended and agent actions, Otterly.AI has GEO audits and recommendations, Scrunch has page optimizations, and Ahrefs has its Letaido agent. "Dashboard only" is no longer the dividing line. The useful questions are who approves a change before it ships, and whether the tool re-runs the same prompts afterward to check whether it worked.

DIY 5-prompt audit you can run in the next 10 minutes

Before you buy any of these tools, run this audit yourself. In 10 minutes it will tell you whether your brand has the kind of visibility problem that needs a tracker.

Open ChatGPT and Perplexity (free accounts are fine) in a fresh chat with memory and personalization off. Run each of these 5 prompts in both engines, substituting your category, brand, persona, and a real competitor. The examples use a neutral category so you can see the pattern.

  • "Best [category] for [persona] in 2026, with pros and cons of each option." Example: "Best cloud data warehouses for a 50-person analytics team in 2026, with pros and cons of each option." This is the canonical category-discovery prompt. If you're not among the first 5 brands named, you have a category-visibility problem.
  • "[Your brand] vs. [closest competitor]: which is better for [use case]?" Example: "Snowflake vs. Databricks: which is better for a team that mostly runs SQL dashboards?" This tests whether you or your competitor controls the comparison narrative.
  • "[Closest competitor] alternatives: what are 5 other tools to consider?" Example: "Databricks alternatives: what are 5 other platforms to consider?" You want your own brand in that list. If you're absent, the engine doesn't put you in that competitor's competitive set.
  • "What does [your brand] do, and who is it for?" This tests what the engine already knows about you. If the answer is wrong, outdated, or generic, your About page, homepage, and the third-party pages that describe you aren't doing their job.
  • "Show me the most recent independent review of [your brand]." This tests source-layer trust. If the engine returns nothing, or only your own pages, your third-party coverage is too thin.

Record the results, then re-run

For each prompt in each engine (10 runs), capture: (a) was your brand mentioned, (b) was it recommended or just listed, (c) which URLs were cited, and (d) did the answer get any fact wrong about you. Put the results in a spreadsheet; that's your baseline.

Then run the same 5 prompts again 24 hours later. If the answers shift, you've confirmed the variance problem at the top of this article, and made the case for a scheduled tracker over a one-shot tool.

Illustrative scenario: same mention rate, different outcome

Illustrative scenario only. A mid-market data-warehouse vendor is named in 31% of ChatGPT runs and 29% of Perplexity runs on the same 40 prompts. On ChatGPT, most mentions come with a caveat about pricing complexity; on Perplexity, the brand is usually the recommended pick for SQL-heavy teams. Similar mention rates, very different effect on a buyer reading the answer. Track mention rate alone and you miss the framing problem entirely.

FAQ

How often does ChatGPT refresh what it knows about my brand?

It depends on the path. When ChatGPT searches the web, it can use a page once OpenAI's search crawler (OAI-SearchBot) has reached it, so make sure robots.txt allows it; OpenAI says robots.txt changes take about 24 hours to register. When ChatGPT answers from model memory, new facts arrive only with a new model, and OpenAI changed ChatGPT's default model several times in 2026. A page you publish today can appear in search answers soon after it's crawled, and in memory-based answers only if a later model learns it.

Does ChatGPT with search differ from ChatGPT answering from memory?

Yes. With search, ChatGPT retrieves live pages and cites them. From memory, it answers from training data, doesn't browse, and can invent URLs. ChatGPT decides per question which path to take, and users can also turn search on. A tracker should record the cited sources for each answer so you can tell which path produced it.

What's the difference between Perplexity and Perplexity Pro for brand tracking?

Both build answers from live web retrieval and cite their sources. Pro and Max subscribers can choose among models from several labs and run more Pro Searches; free users get a limited number of Pro Searches and can't pick the model. Most trackers, SolCrys included, measure one consistent Perplexity configuration. If paid users dominate your buyer mix, spot-check their answers by hand.

Can I track ChatGPT brand mentions without paying for a tool?

For a small prompt set (5–25 prompts, weekly), yes: the DIY audit above plus a spreadsheet works. The bottleneck is consistency. Most teams run the audit once, see the result, and never run it again, so they have no baseline to detect change against. The reason to pay for a tool is the scheduled re-run plus the cited-source capture, not the single snapshot.

Does Perplexity show different results in different countries?

It can. Perplexity searches the web in real time and results can be localized, so the same prompt can return different sources in the US and the EU. Pick the markets that matter to your buyers and measure from those; in SolCrys, each workspace has its own measurement country.

Do I need a separate tracker for ChatGPT and one for Perplexity?

No, but check the plan. Every tool in the table covers both engines somewhere, though not always at the entry tier: Perplexity is an add-on on Peec AI's self-serve plans, weekly-only on Semrush, Enterprise- or agency-only on Profound, and on Pro or Custom at SolCrys. The reason to use more than one tool is cross-checking (see Why Two AEO Platforms Can Disagree), not engine coverage.

Is "AI brand visibility" the same as "AI share of voice"?

Closely related but not identical. AI brand visibility usually means "does the engine say your name at all on relevant prompts" (mention rate). AI share of voice is the percentage of total relevant answer space your brand occupies versus competitors. A brand can have high visibility and low share of voice (mentioned but always buried), or the reverse. The simplest way to think about it: visibility is presence, share of voice is dominance.

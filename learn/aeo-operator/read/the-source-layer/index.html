The source layer: owned, editorial, community

Different source types behave differently, respond to different work, and cannot substitute for each other.

Four layers, four different jobs

Sort your cited domains into owned (yours), competitor, editorial (publications, analysts, reviewers), and community (forums, discussion sites, user-generated content). Each behaves differently and each is influenced by a different kind of work.

Owned is the layer you control completely and the layer engines trust least on its own, because a claim on your own site is an assertion. Editorial is slow to move and heavily weighted. Community is fast, volatile, and frequently the largest single share of citations in a technical or software category — and it is also where the most brand-damaging misinformation lives.

Owned content is not dismissed, it is misunderstood

It is common to see the owned share of citations reported as a small percentage and concluded from that that owned content does not matter. That is a misreading. Any single domain holding a meaningful share of a category's citations is a strong position, because the denominator is every source on the open web.

More importantly, the layers do different jobs in one funnel. Third-party sources are what make an engine willing to say your name. Your own pages are what the engine reads to get your facts right, and what converts the buyer who clicks through. Trying to substitute one for the other is how programmes end up either invisible or unable to convert the visibility they earned.

Match the work to the layer

The layer distribution in your citations tells you where the work is. If editorial dominates your category, your gap is a coverage and relationships problem. If community dominates, your gap is a participation problem, and the only ethical way in is to be genuinely useful in those communities rather than to plant mentions.

If your own domain is absent even from prompts about your own brand, you have a technical or content problem on your site, which Module 4 will help you localise.

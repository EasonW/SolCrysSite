Reading the citations

The list of sources an engine cited is the most actionable artefact you have, because it names exactly which pages you would need to be on.

Citations are a map of what to influence

When an engine composes an answer about your category, it draws on specific URLs. That list is not trivia. It is a directory of the pages that currently shape what buyers are told, and therefore the concrete targets for any off-domain work.

Read them as a set, not individually. Look for the pages that recur across many prompts — those are the load-bearing sources in your category, and presence on one of them is worth more than presence on a dozen one-off mentions.

Cited does not mean the page mentions you

A subtle and important distinction: an engine can cite a page that has nothing to say about your brand. The citation tells you the engine used that page to answer the question, not that the page endorsed you.

So a page cited on a prompt you lose is a target — being covered there could change the answer. A page cited on a prompt you win, which does mention you, is an asset — worth protecting and worth understanding, because whatever it says about you is currently working.

Why you ship exactly one change

Shipping five changes at once feels productive and destroys your ability to learn anything from the result.

Attribution requires restraint

If you fix the page, publish two new articles, get a review site listing, and update your schema in the same week, and the number moves, you have learned that something worked. You have not learned what, so you cannot do it again on purpose.

One change per cycle is slower per week and much faster per lesson learned. After three cycles the team that changed one thing at a time knows three things. The team that changed fifteen things knows that their numbers went up.

This is genuinely hard to hold to under pressure to show activity. The defence is to be explicit that the cycle is an experiment with a stated hypothesis, not a sprint of content output.

Write the hypothesis before you ship

State it in one sentence: because the engine is failing at [mode], I am changing [thing], and I expect [specific prompt] to show [specific movement] by [date].

A hypothesis that cannot fail is not one. "This will improve our AI visibility" is unfalsifiable. "This should make us appear in the answer to prompt 4, which we currently lose to a named competitor" can be checked, and can be wrong, which is what makes it worth testing.

Write down the date you will re-measure, and make it at least two weeks out.

Brand facts and the competitive set

Naming your real competitors is a measurement decision, not a marketing one — and getting it wrong quietly corrupts every comparison you make afterwards.

Name the competitors buyers name

The competitive set you track should be the one buyers actually mention, not the one your positioning deck aspires to. These are frequently different, and the difference matters because share-of-voice against a set that does not reflect the real consideration set is a number that cannot inform any decision.

The quickest way to get this right is to ask a few recent buyers what else they looked at, or read the alternatives your own sales calls surface. Then add anyone an answer engine names when asked about your category, even if you do not consider them a real competitor — if the engine names them to buyers, they are in your consideration set whether you like it or not.

Brand facts versus Corporate Context

Corporate Context is the narrative substrate. Brand facts are the discrete, checkable statements you want available for grounding — the specific numbers, dates, capabilities, and claims.

Keep them separate in your head: context teaches an engine who you are, facts give it things it can quote. Facts can be extracted from documents and URLs and then curated, which is usually faster than writing them from scratch, but curate them properly — an extracted fact that is stale or out of context is a wrong answer waiting to be repeated.

Why context comes before measurement

You cannot grade an AI answer as right or wrong without a written statement of what is true. Corporate Context is that statement, and it has to exist before the first run.

Accuracy needs a reference

It is easy to measure whether an engine mentioned you. It is much harder, and much more valuable, to measure whether what it said was correct. That second measurement is impossible without a written source of truth to compare against.

This is the reason context comes first in this course rather than as a later refinement. A baseline taken without a reference statement can only tell you presence. A baseline taken with one can tell you presence and accuracy, and accuracy is usually where the actionable problem is hiding.

What belongs in it, and what does not

Corporate Context is not marketing copy. It is the factual substrate: what the product does, who it is for, what it costs, what it does not do, how it differs from the named alternatives, and which claims you can substantiate.

The instinct to write it persuasively is the main failure. Persuasive copy is full of unfalsifiable claims — "the leading platform," "trusted by teams everywhere" — and an unfalsifiable claim cannot be used to grade an answer. If an engine said it, you could not mark it right or wrong. Write things that could be checked.

The second failure is silence. Whatever your context does not mention cannot be graded, so a gap in the document becomes a blind spot in every accuracy measurement that follows. If pricing is not in there, you will never learn that engines are quoting a price you retired two years ago.

  • Include: what it is, who it serves, concrete capabilities, pricing structure, named alternatives and honest differences, substantiable proof points
  • Include: the things you are commonly confused with — a same-name company, an adjacent category, a product you sunset
  • Exclude: superlatives, unfalsifiable positioning, anything you would not want an engine to repeat verbatim

It is organization-level, not per-workspace

One practical detail that surprises people: Corporate Context is set at the organization level and shared across every workspace underneath it. It is not a per-workspace field you can vary by campaign.

That is deliberate — the whole point is one canonical statement of brand truth rather than several drifting ones — but it means if you manage several distinct brands, they need separate organizations rather than separate workspaces.
